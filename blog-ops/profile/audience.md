# Audience

## Primary audience

Students, early-career professionals, and aspiring bloggers/creators who
feel stretched thin and want to get more done without burning out. They're
curious about AI and productivity tools but skeptical of hype — they want to
know what actually works from someone who has used it. Many are, like Olga
herself, juggling work or study with a side project and looking to reclaim
their time and keep learning.

## Secondary audience

Busy knowledge workers and self-improvers who don't identify as "bloggers"
but land here searching for a specific productivity technique or tool (e.g.
timeboxing, planning tips, things to do instead of scrolling).

## Pain points (priority order)

1. Feeling overwhelmed and short on time — too much to do, not enough hours
2. Losing focus to distraction, especially the phone / endless scrolling
3. Tool overwhelm — not knowing which AI or productivity tools are actually
   worth adopting versus hype
4. Intimidation by anything that looks "technical" (building a blog, setting
   up tools, scary-sounding acronyms) that stops them getting started
5. Inconsistent planning and procrastination — good intentions that don't
   turn into follow-through

## Language to use

- Warm, encouraging, first-person ("From personal experience...", "Here's
  how I...")
- "Reclaim your time," "automate the mundane," "elevate the exciting"
- Plain, concrete, tested-from-experience framing
- Short reassuring asides that lower the barrier to starting

## Language to avoid

- Toxic hustle-culture / "grind harder" messaging
- Unexplained jargon or acronym dumps
- Overhyped "this one trick changes everything" claims
- Cold, corporate, or academic tone

## Niche jargon translation

| Insider term | Reader-facing translation |
|---|---|
| SERP | the page of results you get after a Google search |
| DR (Domain Rating) | a score for how trusted a website is in Google's eyes |
| Keyword difficulty | how hard it is to rank on Google for a given search term |
| Parkinson's Law | work expands to fill the time you give it |
| Timeboxing | giving a task a fixed time slot and stopping when it's up |
| Kanban board | a board of columns (to-do / doing / done) you move cards across |

## Reading posture

Translate niche jargon on first use — the typical reader is a curious
beginner, not an insider, so every specialist term gets a plain-language
explanation the first time it appears.
