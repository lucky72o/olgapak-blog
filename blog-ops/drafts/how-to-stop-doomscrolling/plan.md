## Meta description (draft)

`Doomscrolling isn't a willpower problem—it's a design problem. Here are 12 science-backed, same-day ways to stop, without deleting your apps.` (137 chars)

## Angle

Doomscrolling isn't a character flaw or a willpower failure — the apps are engineered to hijack your attention, so "failing" after a few days is the expected outcome, not proof you're weak. This post opens by killing that guilt and explaining what your brain is actually doing (dopamine/variable-reward loops + negativity bias), then hands over 12 concrete, same-day strategies grouped by how they work — reshape your environment, catch the trigger, and rebuild your routine — with the single most-repeated real-world insight built in as the spine: replace, don't just remove (deleting Instagram only moves the scroll to Reddit). It's the non-shaming, environment-first, better-sourced take the health-authority explainers and dated tip-lists don't deliver — and it never tells you to quit your phone.

## Key sections (preview, becomes the outline in Phase 2)

1. What doomscrolling actually is (and why "just stop" never works) — short definitional + guilt-killer + the brain mechanism (dopamine/variable rewards + negativity bias), science-backed
2. The 12 strategies, grouped into three layers:
   - **Reshape your environment (make the scroll harder to start):** 1) Move the feeds off your home screen; 2) Make your phone boring — grayscale + kill notifications; 3) Charge your phone outside the bedroom; 4) Keep messaging, cut the feeds (the "without deactivating" answer); 5) Use time limits + a delay app as a speed bump (honest: timers alone get bypassed)
   - **Catch the trigger (work with your brain, not against it):** 6) Name the feeling before you unlock (urge-surfing); 7) Replace, don't just remove — always have something to reach for; 8) Stand up and move — the 30-second pattern interrupt
   - **Rebuild your routine (design days that don't need willpower):** 9) Protect a phone-free first hour; 10) Trade the phone alarm/clock for real ones (watch + alarm clock); 11) The "two sources, two times a day" news diet; 12) Be kind when you slip — relapse is designed in
3. Closing / CTA (reclaim the time — soft free-AI-tools mention)
4. FAQ

(Structural note: section 2's three layers may render as three H2s each with H3 strategies, or a single "12 strategies" H2 with 12 H3s — the outline decides. Either way the reader gets exactly 12 numbered, labeled strategies.)
