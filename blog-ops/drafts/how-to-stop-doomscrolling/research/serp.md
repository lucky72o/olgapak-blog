## Audience inferences

- Primary audience: students / early-career people who lose hours to reels + news and feel guilty about it (matches brief). The Dr Gina Cleo confessional voice and Reddit rank-1 thread confirm this is a self-aware, self-critical reader who has *already tried* time limits and app deletion.
- Secondary audience: knowledge-workers / self-improvers searching the exact technique; and an ADHD/distraction-prone slice (Health Assured devotes a section to it; the Reddit r/ADHD thread exists).
- Reader knowledge level: beginner-to-intermediate — knows the term, wants mechanism + tactics that actually stick.
- Reader intent: informational, problem_solution.

## Angle opportunities

- **Cover reels/TikTok/entertainment doomscrolling, not just "the news."** Every health-authority page (MHF, Nuffield, Happiful) frames doomscrolling as negative-news consumption. The blog's audience mostly doomscrolls reels/comparison/FOMO content — meet them there.
- **Actually explain the brain mechanism with cited science.** Only Health Assured and Nuffield gesture at dopamine/negativity bias, and Health Assured's stats are uncited/dubious. A clearly-sourced "why your brain does this: dopamine loops, variable rewards, negativity bias" section is an open lane (the harvested primary sources below make it doable).
- **Lead with "it's environment/design, not willpower" and stay non-shaming.** The SERP hands readers tip-lists; none open by dismantling the guilt. Dr Gina Cleo and Zen Habits gesture at it — the blog can own it.
- **"Replace, don't just remove."** The single most-repeated real-world insight (Zen Habits on the SERP; overwhelmingly on Reddit) — deleting an app just moves the scroll. Few SERP tip-lists foreground this.
- **12 distinct, same-day tactics grouped by type (environment / mindset / routine).** No single competitor combines all three layers into one coherent, numbered, science-backed set — the brief's exact spec.
- **Freshness.** Happiful and MHF still carry COVID-era framing; a 2026 post can feel current.

## Avoid pitfalls

- **Don't present "turn off notifications / set a time limit / grayscale your screen" as novel** — they appear in nearly every result. Include them, but briefly and with the *why*, not as headline reveals.
- **Don't preach digital detox / going off-grid.** The SERP itself (Guardian, Dr Gina Cleo) pushes *against* the nuclear option; readers explicitly search "without quitting your phone."
- **Don't frame doomscrolling as only-about-the-news** — it dates the piece and misses the reels/FOMO reality.
- **Don't ship uncited "science."** The competitors' weakest move ("IQ drops 10 points") is exactly what a "science-backed" post must not repeat.
