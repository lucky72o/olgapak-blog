## Body sections

### H2 1: `What doomscrolling actually is (and why "just stop" never works)`

- Bullet: Short, warm definition of doomscrolling (compulsively scrolling negative/comparison/FOMO content, reels and news alike, long past the point it feels good); normalize it, kill the guilt up front.
- Bullet: The mechanism, science-backed: variable-reward/dopamine loops + negativity bias make the feed feel urgent; the apps are engineered for it, so "failing" after a few days is the expected outcome, not a character flaw. Mark `[EXTERNAL_LINK_NEEDED: dopamine variable-reward loops + negativity bias keep you scrolling, suggested source: APA or NLM/PubMed]` for Stage 3d.
- Bullet: Key fact to cite: doomscrolling is associated with psychological distress, `Anand et al 2021`, source: https://doi.org/10.1111/ppc.12803 (the "it genuinely makes you feel worse" evidence).
- Bullet: Internal link (from intro P3 narrative continues): reassure this is about reclaiming attention to `stay focused on what actually matters` (how-to-stay-focused-on-goals) — if not placed in intro, place here.
- Bullet: Reframe line (VOC): deleting the app just moves the scroll; the fix is design + replacement, not discipline (sets up the 3 layers).
- Approximate word count: ~330

### H2 2: `Layer 1: Reshape your environment (make the scroll harder to start)`

- Bullet: One-paragraph frame: the highest-leverage change is friction, not willpower; make the phone boring and the feeds a few steps further away. (VOC: "you're an addict trying to stop drinking, at a bar.")
- Bullet: Key fact for the layer: grayscale "removes positive reinforcements" — Tristan Harris, source: https://www.wired.com/story/grayscale-ios-android-smartphone-addiction/
- Approximate word count: ~120 frame + 5 × ~130 = ~770

#### H3 1.1: `1. Move the feeds off your home screen`
- Bullet: Bury social/news apps in a folder on the last page (or delete the icon, keep the account) so opening one takes a deliberate search, not a thumb reflex; this buys you a pause. VOC: r/ADHD "deliberate search instead of a thumb reflex."

#### H3 1.2: `2. Make your phone boring: grayscale + kill notifications`
- Bullet: Turn on grayscale (removes the color rewards) and turn off all non-human notifications (no red badges pulling you back). Cite Wired/Tristan Harris.

#### H3 1.3: `3. Charge your phone outside the bedroom`
- Bullet: The bedroom is where the worst scrolling happens (morning + bedtime). Charge it in another room; use a real alarm clock (setup for H3 10). Science: blue light suppresses melatonin (`https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29101797/`) and in-bed social-media use is linked to insomnia & daytime sleepiness (Bhat 2018, `https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleh.2017.12.004`).

#### H3 1.4: `4. Keep messaging, cut the feeds (you don't have to deactivate)`
- Bullet: Answers the literal top reader ask ("without deactivating"). Keep WhatsApp/iMessage/DMs for real connection; remove or bury the infinite feeds. Internal link: `productive things to do on your phone instead of scrolling` (productive-things-to-do-on-your-phone-instead-of-scrolling). VOC: "Uninstall social media. Keep messaging apps."

#### H3 1.5: `5. Use time limits and a delay app as a speed bump, not a wall`
- Bullet: Honest caveat: pure timers get bypassed/reinstalled (VOC). Use built-in Screen Time limits + a one-tap "wait 10 seconds" delay app as friction that interrupts autopilot, and accept they're a speed bump, not a lock.

### H2 3: `Layer 2: Catch the trigger (work with your brain, not against it)`

- Bullet: One-paragraph frame: doomscrolling is usually a coping reflex for a feeling (boredom, stress, sadness, avoidance). Catch the feeling and you can redirect before autopilot takes over. VOC: "for me it's boredom plus avoidance."
- Bullet: Key fact: framed via the "become a scientist studying your habits, not judgmental" mindset (Leo Babauta / Zen Habits — attribute as expert framing, don't link the competitor).
- Approximate word count: ~110 frame + 3 × ~140 = ~530

#### H3 3.1: `6. Name the feeling before you unlock`
- Bullet: A 5-second pause: "what do I actually want right now?" Name the emotion (bored/anxious/lonely). Naming it (urge-surfing) loosens the reflex. VOC + Dr Gina Cleo "what do I actually want right now?"

#### H3 3.2: `7. Replace, don't just remove`
- Bullet: THE spine strategy: deleting an app without a replacement just moves the scroll (VOC: "deleted Instagram, now I scroll Reddit all day"). Keep a specific alternative in reach — a book/Kindle, an audiobook, a walk, a project. Internal link: `things to do instead of being on your phone` (things-to-do-instead-of-being-on-your-phone). VOC: "you can't just STOP, you have to START projects."

#### H3 3.3: `8. Stand up and move: the 30-second pattern interrupt`
- Bullet: When you catch yourself mid-scroll, physically stand and move for 30 seconds (water, window, stretch). Breaks the loop cheaply. VOC: "just standing up and walking around a little refreshes my brain"; Happiful's say-"stop"+move technique.

### H2 4: `Layer 3: Rebuild your routine (design days that don't need willpower)`

- Bullet: One-paragraph frame: the durable fix is routines where the phone isn't the default — bookend your day phone-free and give the scroll less airtime by design.
- Bullet: Key fact: environment/routine over discipline — Dr Gina Cleo "I stopped by redesigning my habits, environment, and routines," (attribute; competitor URL not linked).
- Approximate word count: ~110 frame + 4 × ~130 = ~630

#### H3 4.1: `9. Protect a phone-free first hour`
- Bullet: How you start sets the day's tone; no scroll before you're up and moving. Pair with a plan for the morning. Internal link: `timeboxing your day` (what-is-timeboxing) as the "fill the time with intention" move. VOC: charging phone in another room "breaks the morning scroll which sets the tone for the whole day."

#### H3 4.2: `10. Trade your phone alarm and clock for real ones`
- Bullet: A cheap alarm clock + a wristwatch removes the two "innocent" reasons you reach for the phone (time + alarm) that turn into a scroll. MHF/Nuffield/Dr Gina Cleo all recommend it.

#### H3 4.3: `11. Try the "two sources, two times a day" news diet`
- Bullet: For the news/"civic duty" doomscroller: pick two good-quality sources, check them at two set times, done. Staying informed doesn't require a 24/7 drip. Attribute the "Two by Two approach" to psychologist Kimberley Wilson (expert technique, no competitor link). VOC: the "civic duty to stay informed" trap.

#### H3 4.4: `12. Be kind when you slip (relapse is designed in)`
- Bullet: The closer + guilt-killer bookend: you will slip; the apps are built to make you. Treat a lapse as data, not failure, and restart the next tactic. VOC: "at least I stopped thinking it's a personal flaw." Zen Habits self-compassion.

## FAQ block

- Q: `What is doomscrolling, exactly?` → A direction: one-sentence plain definition (compulsively scrolling negative/FOMO content long past the point it feels good), non-clinical.
- Q: `How do I stop doomscrolling without deleting my apps?` → A direction: friction + replacement (bury the feeds, keep messaging, keep a real alternative in reach) — you don't need to deactivate.
- Q: `Why can't I stop doomscrolling even when I want to?` → A direction: it's engineered (variable-reward loops + negativity bias), so it's a design problem, not a willpower or character problem.
- Q: `Is doomscrolling actually bad for you?` → A direction: yes — linked to psychological distress and, at night, worse sleep (blue light/melatonin); brief, sourced, not alarmist.
- Q: `What should I do instead of doomscrolling?` → A direction: a specific replacement you keep in reach (book, walk, audiobook, a project) — link the "things to do instead" post.
