## Handwriting vs. typing research (the most over-cited claim in this niche)

**Mueller, P. A., & Oppenheimer, D. M. (2014). "The pen is mightier than the keyboard: advantages of longhand over laptop note taking." Psychological Science, 25(6), 1159–1168.**
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24760141/ (NLM, allowlist-class)
Abstract read verbatim on 2026-08-19. Exact scope, do NOT overclaim past this:
- "In three studies, we found that students who took notes on laptops performed worse on **conceptual questions** than students who took notes longhand." Conceptual questions specifically, not recall generally.
- Mechanism claimed: "laptop note takers' tendency to **transcribe lectures verbatim** rather than processing information and reframing it in their own words is detrimental to learning."
- The study compares LONGHAND ON PAPER vs. LAPTOP KEYBOARD. It says nothing about tablets or stylus handwriting. Any transfer to an iPad is an inference about the *transcription* mechanism, and the post must say so rather than implying the study tested iPads.

**Morehead, K., Dunlosky, J., & Rawson, K. A. (2019). "How Much Mightier Is the Pen than the Keyboard for Note-Taking? A Replication and Extension of Mueller and Oppenheimer (2014)." Educational Psychology Review.**
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09468-2 (peer-reviewed journal, allowlist-class by the blog-craft.md heuristic)
Abstract read verbatim on 2026-08-19:
- Direct replication plus eWriter and no-notes groups. "performance did not consistently differ between any groups (experiments 1 and 2), including a group who did not take notes."
- "A meta-analysis (combining direct replications) of test performance revealed **small (nonsignificant) effects favoring longhand**."
- Authors' own conclusion: "concluding which method is superior for improving the functions of note-taking seems **premature**."

**Editorial consequence:** the post must NOT claim handwriting beats typing for memory as settled fact. The honest, defensible line is: the well-known 2014 study found a conceptual-question gap and blamed verbatim transcription, and a 2019 direct replication did not reproduce a reliable difference. What survives both is the *mechanism*: copying words down without processing them is the thing that hurts, whichever tool you hold. That framing also happens to be the post's actual argument (the app doesn't save you from bad notes), so it costs nothing to be accurate here.

## Apple documentation (allowlist-class, correct citation for every stock-iPad feature claim)

**Apple Pencil compatibility + features:** https://www.apple.com/apple-pencil/
Read 2026-08-19. Confirms on-page: palm rejection, tilt sensitivity, low latency, and pixel-perfect precision across the line; Apple Pencil Pro adds pressure sensitivity, squeeze, barrel roll, haptic feedback, double-tap, wireless pairing/charging and Find My, none of which Apple Pencil (USB-C) has. Carries a "Find the Apple Pencil that works with your iPad. Select your iPad model" picker covering every model back to iPad (6th generation) / iPad mini (5th generation) — this is the right link for the compatibility question instead of the post hard-coding a matrix that goes stale. Apple Pencil Pro compatibility is limited to iPad Pro (M4/M5), iPad Air (M2/M3/M4) and iPad mini (A17 Pro); Apple Pencil (USB-C) covers a much wider list including iPad (10th gen) and iPad (A16).
**Prices are on that page and must not be repeated in the post.**

**Scribble (handwriting converted to typed text):** https://support.apple.com/guide/ipad/enter-text-with-scribble-ipad355ab2a7/ipados
Read 2026-08-19 (geo-redirects to /en-gb/ for this browser; the geo-neutral form above is the one to link). Confirms: "Scribble converts your handwriting to text directly on your iPad, so your writing stays private." Works in any text field, and in Notes/Freeform via the Handwriting tool to the left of the pen. Scratch out to delete, circle or underline to select, draw a vertical line to join/separate. Turned off at Settings > Apple Pencil > Scribble. Availability varies by language.

**Handwriting in Notes + handwriting SEARCH:** https://support.apple.com/guide/ipad/add-drawings-and-handwriting-ipada87a6078/ipados
Read 2026-08-19. Confirms: you can draw or write with Apple Pencil (supported models) **or your finger**; "As you write with Apple Pencil, your handwriting can be automatically refined in real time to be more legible while maintaining the look and feel of your own handwriting style"; and, verbatim, "**You can search handwritten text (in supported languages) in Notes.**" Also: if a note has no title, the first line of handwritten text becomes the suggested title; Smart Selection lets you select handwriting with the same gestures as typed text and paste it elsewhere as typed text. This is the citation for the single biggest genuine iPad-over-paper advantage the post makes.

**Notes app overview (Quick Notes, scanning, audio, Markup):** https://support.apple.com/guide/ipad/get-started-with-notes-ipadfeea407c/ipados
Already deep-fetched as `03-support-apple-com.json`. Confirms Quick Notes from any app or screen (swipe up from the bottom-right corner with Apple Pencil), document scanning, audio recording, Markup tools and the ruler, and iCloud sync across devices.
