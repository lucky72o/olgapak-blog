## Angle

Every post ranking for this keyword teaches you how to build a beautiful iPad note system, and none of them mentions that the most-upvoted thing students actually say about iPads is that theirs ended up as an expensive third device between the phone and the laptop. This post gives the full working setup — one app, a folder structure, a handwriting-or-typing rule, a capture method, a review habit — and is honest at every step about which parts are optional, which are traps, and when a paper notebook is still the right answer. It is the setup guide written by someone who assumes you will get bored of it in three weeks and builds for that.

## Key sections (preview, becomes the outline in Phase 2)

1. First, be honest about whether the iPad is the right tool
2. Step 1: Pick one app and stop shopping
3. Step 2: Set up your notebooks before your first lecture
4. Step 3: Decide when you handwrite and when you type
5. Step 4: Take the notes, then actually review them
   - H3 sub-step: the review pass (this is where handwriting search earns the iPad its keep)
6. The gear that earns its place (and the gear that doesn't)
7. The mistakes that turn an iPad into an expensive Netflix screen
8. Closing CTA
9. FAQ

Seven body H2s. Capture and review were merged at plan review (Stage 1c.5, Issue 2): they are two short sequential beats of the same numbered method, and merging them buys the apps section the room it needs to label six apps honestly. Sections 1 and 7 stay separate on purpose — they are the angle's bookends and collapsing them would remove the exact thing that differentiates this post from a uniformly pro-iPad SERP.

**Sections that rest on the author's own methodology rather than a research citation:** H2 3 (notebook and folder setup) and the review sub-step under H2 5 are Olga's tested practice, not research findings, and neither needs a sourced claim to justify it. Both still have concrete `facts.md` anchors available where a detail helps — H2 3 can use the Apple organizational facts (iCloud sync across devices, an untitled note taking its title from the first line of handwriting, Quick Notes capture from any screen) and the review sub-step rests on handwriting search — so the outline's "every H2 cites ≥1 fact" rule is satisfied without inventing a `[VERIFY:]`-worthy claim in either.

Apps named inside section 2, labelled by fit rather than ranked (no "best app" verdict — the research does not support one):
- Apple Notes, free and already installed, for anyone who wants to start today
- GoodNotes, the all-rounder, with the subscription and reliability objections stated honestly
- Notability, the student pick, audio recorded against the notes
- Noteful / CollaNote, the no-subscription alternatives readers volunteer
- OneNote, for people living in Microsoft
- Notion / Obsidian, typed and structured rather than handwritten (absent from every SERP result — the gap this post fills)

## Tone hooks

- Concede early. The post earns the right to give instructions by first admitting the iPad is not automatically better than paper — the research is unambiguous that this is what readers actually want addressed.
- Beginner-safe vocabulary. Translate palm rejection, paper-feel screen protector, Scribble, handwriting search and PDF annotation on first use, per `audience.md` §Reading posture.
- Name the traps as traps, warmly. "Spent a fortune on templates" is a real person, not a strawman; the tone is "I've done this too", not "don't be that person".
- No app coronation. Readers disagree loudly about which app wins and the research supports no single answer; label by fit and say so out loud.
- Olga's honest position: she has written the paper-notebook and paper-method posts on this site. The credible framing is someone who kept paper for some things and moved others across, not a convert.
