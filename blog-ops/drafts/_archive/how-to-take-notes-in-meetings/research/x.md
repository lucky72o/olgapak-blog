## Industry-take themes

- **Theme:** In-person, multi-person meetings remain an unsolved note-taking/transcription problem even in a market saturated with AI notetakers.
  - Evidence: "Why has nobody solved for in-person meeting note-taking? If someone has, I'm ready to buy", @kabirnagral, source: https://x.com/kabirnagral/status/2094652022914203656
  - Evidence: "phone mics don't work well for multiple people in a meeting... I think this might be more of a mic and hardware problem to solve", @kabirnagral, same URL

- **Theme:** Manual transcription and active listening are framed as being in direct competition for attention, not complementary.
  - Evidence: "while I was busy writing down what you said 30 seconds ago, I was missing what you're saying right now... For me, note-taking was never really listening. It was a safety net. Pen-shaped.", @DrPengSHEN, source: https://x.com/DrPengSHEN/status/2096437717320278027

- **Theme:** Even among people building or promoting AI notetaking products, the "AI replaces your notes entirely" framing is not universal, some position AI as a backup to your own notes rather than a replacement.
  - Evidence: "You take your own notes while AI listens in the background, then helps you catch what you missed or misunderstood.", @onlyonepsalm, source: https://x.com/onlyonepsalm/status/2092309012549169638

## Angle opportunities for this blog

- The @DrPengSHEN post is nearly a verbatim match for this post's planned thesis ("transcription crowds out listening"). The post can use this as a real-world data point that the "capture vs. decide" failure mode isn't unique to this blog's framing, it's independently observed and getting real engagement on X, while still steering the reader toward the post's actual recommendation (a capture structure, not full transcription-by-hand or full AI outsourcing).

Frame it plainly: AI notetaking's blind spot is exactly the in-person/multi-person case, where this post's manual template matters most.
