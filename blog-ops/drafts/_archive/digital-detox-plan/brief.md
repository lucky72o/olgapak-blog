## What the human wants to convey

- The content-plan angle: an INFO guide to a **digital detox plan** structured as a 7-day
  screen-time reset the reader can start today, one small change per day.
- A detox is not a punishment or a phone-in-a-drawer week. It's a reset that makes the phone
  boring on purpose so attention goes back to work that matters (per `blog.md` §Point of view).
- Practical and tested, not theorized: concrete daily actions, a realistic time cost, and what the
  first uncomfortable days actually feel like.
- Cover the failure mode most detox posts skip: the all-or-nothing cold-turkey week that collapses
  by Wednesday and leaves the reader back where they started (often worse, because now they think
  they failed). The plan needs ramps, allowances, and a "what to do when you slip" step.
- Say what happens after day 7 — the plan is worthless if screen time snaps back on day 8, so the
  post ends with the keep-it-going rules, not a finish line.
- Weave in what this blog already owns (doomscrolling triggers, phone-free alternatives, planning
  and timeboxing) rather than repeating them wholesale — link across instead.

## Avoid list

- No cold-turkey / "delete all your apps" absolutism, and no shame framing about screen time.
- No hustle-culture "you're wasting your life" moralizing (per `audience.md` §Language to avoid).
- No overhyped "this one trick rewires your brain" claims; neuroscience claims need a real source
  or they don't ship.
- No invented statistics. Every screen-time or attention number traces to `facts.md` or carries a
  `[VERIFY:]` marker with an honest source clause.
- No product hard-sell; no prices (the tools are free and there is no pricing page).
- No affiliate links and no affiliate disclosure (the Associates program is not live).
