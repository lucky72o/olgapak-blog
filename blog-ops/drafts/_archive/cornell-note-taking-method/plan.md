## Meta description (draft)

`Learn the Cornell note-taking method step by step: the three-zone layout, a filled-in worked example, when to use it (and when not), plus a free template.`

## Angle

Every competitor describes the three-zone layout, but not one shows a real filled-in page, tells you when NOT to use Cornell, or treats the "does it actually work?" question honestly. This pillar opens with the exact skepticism real students voice ("is it worth it, or just a fancy trend?"), then earns trust by being the only guide that combines a concrete worked example, honest cited evidence (mixed results, not superlatives), clear when-to-use / when-to-switch guidance, and a grabbable printable + Notion template — serving students and professionals in one hub the whole note-taking cluster links back to.

## Tone hooks

- Open by naming the real skepticism ("is it worth it, or just a fancy trend?") and answer it honestly — don't lead with a superlative.
- Make the summary row the hero; address the "I'm too lazy to do the summary" friction head-on (that's where the Text Summarizer tie-in earns its place).
- Beginner-friendly: translate every study term (cue column, active recall, the 5 R's) on first use; keep the bar to "doable today" low.
- Honest and cited on effectiveness — no "best way to take notes" claims; hedge the handwriting-vs-typing claim (no primary source in our research).
