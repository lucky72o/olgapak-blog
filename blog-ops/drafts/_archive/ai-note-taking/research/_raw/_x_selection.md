# X selection: ai note taking

Tab used: **Top** (`f=top`). Kept rather than re-pulling `f=live`: Top is heavily commercial, but it
still contains four genuinely high-engagement practitioner threads, and the Latest tab on this query
is even more dominated by app-launch spam. 44 posts captured by auto-scrolling the timeline (X
virtualizes the DOM, so a single snapshot only returns the ~6 visible tweets — the capture scrolls
and accumulates by status URL).

**What the tab actually looks like, and why that matters editorially:** the majority of the 44 are
indie-hacker revenue posts ("AI note taker hits $20K MRR", "another AI note taking app making
MILLIONS", Turbolearn's $300K/month via TikTok) and founder build-in-public promos. That is a real
signal for the post: this category is currently a gold rush, which is exactly why a reader gets
buried in near-identical app recommendations and why an honest "here is what these tools actually do
and where they fail" angle has room.

Selected 5:

1. (rank 17) https://x.com/wojakcodes/status/1968572197296877869 , "do you guys use any AI note
   taking apps?" 403 likes, 80 replies. The single best thread here: an open question that pulled a
   crowd-sourced list of what people genuinely use and what they gave up on. Highest reply count in
   the capture and the closest thing to voice-of-customer on X.
2. (rank 1) https://x.com/rowancheung/status/2095538308436914307 , "AI enhanced journaling is
   changing my life", 197 likes, 55 replies. A concrete personal WORKFLOW (dictation → AI cleanup)
   from a credible AI-newsletter author, not a product pitch. Directly relevant to the
   capture → transcribe → summarize workflow this post recommends.
3. (rank 9) https://x.com/KanikaBK/status/2084628466499231838 , "this 100% free GitHub repo just made
   every AI note-taking app pointless", 237 likes, 19 replies. The local-first / own-your-files
   counterpoint (Obsidian vault + an AI layer instead of a subscription SaaS). Useful as the
   perspective that disagrees with the paid-app consensus, and it touches the privacy angle.
4. (rank 19) https://x.com/heysajib/status/1734906195109183649 , "Google's AI note-taking app just
   got a major upgrade with Gemini... 100% free", 137 likes, 32 replies. The free-tool angle for a
   student audience that will not pay a subscription.
5. (rank 41) https://x.com/Zenith_coder/status/2095430938801123590 , "Google Gemini has powerful
   tools most people never use", 128 likes, 21 replies. A features thread whose Notebook/NotebookLM
   item documents what the free Google option actually does. Weakest of the five on topicality;
   included for the free-tools thread rather than for a point of view.
