## What the human wants to convey

(No human conversation — autopilot. Derived from the content-plan angle + `blog.md` point of view +
`voice.md` positioning.)

- Practical, tested-not-theorized: AI should automate the mundane part of note-taking (transcribing,
  summarizing, tidying) so the reader's attention goes to the thinking part.
- AI does NOT replace the act of taking notes. The retention research behind Cornell / handwriting
  still matters; AI is the assistant, not the note-taker. This is the honest angle that separates
  the post from the "10 best AI note apps" filler.
- A concrete workflow the reader can run the same day, not just a tool list: capture → transcribe →
  summarize → organize → review.
- Name real, current tools honestly, including what each is actually good at and where it falls
  down. No hype, no "this one trick".
- Cover the parts people get burned by: accuracy of transcripts, privacy/consent when recording
  meetings or lectures, and the trap of never re-reading AI summaries.
