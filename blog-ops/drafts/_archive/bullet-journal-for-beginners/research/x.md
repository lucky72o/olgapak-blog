## Industry-take themes

- **Theme: A defined, narrow purpose gets a new notebook actually used; an undefined/general purpose leads to abandonment.**
  - Evidence: "The notebook I bought yesterday is to keep notes on classic literature that I read... I used it for the first time just now for 'Romeo and Juliet'," @jenna_readss, source: https://x.com/jenna_readss/status/2090060047426457941
  - Evidence: "I no longer abandon expensive notebooks after only using 10 pages," once a page-numbering/table-of-contents system was applied, @kellyangyang, source: https://x.com/kellyangyang/status/2091365425322025117

- **Theme: Perfectionism about handwriting/tidiness is a named, common blocker to sticking with a notebook, independent of the bullet journal method specifically.**
  - Evidence: "get demotivated by my messy handwriting, or end up straying from the original purpose and turning it into a chaotic mess that I hate—doesn't anyone else have perfectionist tendencies like that?" @kellyangyang, source: https://x.com/kellyangyang/status/2091365425322025117

- **Theme: Fear of being watched/judged (self-consciousness about "performing" for an imagined audience) is a major, high-engagement blocker to starting any kind of journaling.**
  - Evidence: "the younger crowd on tiktok is saying they struggle with journaling because they don't know how to do it without being performative for anyone/their future self reading it," @yungkalez, source: https://x.com/yungkalez/status/2091213357328060418 (32,227 likes, the single highest-engagement post captured in this research pass across all sources)
  - Evidence: "I struggle with journaling because one time i wrote my biggest insecurities in my journal which my brother then read and proceeded to tease me over," @caioticc (reply), source: same thread

## Counterpoints / contrarian takes

- One un-deep-fetched post is worth surfacing as a single practitioner take per `_x_selection.md`: @JanKutschera (rank 3, 0 likes, 0 replies, posted 2026-08-26): "Most task apps let an ADHD backlog become a museum. The useful part of a bullet journal is harsher: Rewrite every unfinished task tomorrow. Move it forward, send it back, or kill it. A task should earn another day. It should not inherit one." This is the sharpest description of the migration mechanic found in the X data, but per the selection rationale it MUST be cited as a single individual's framing, never presented as a popular or representative view (0 engagement at capture time), source: https://x.com/JanKutschera/status/2092496326910935069 (captured verbatim in `_raw/_x_search.json`, not independently deep-fetched).
