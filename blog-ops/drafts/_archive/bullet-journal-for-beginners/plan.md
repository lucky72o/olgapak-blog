## Angle

Every guide at the top of Google explains the bullet journal as a *system*; almost none of them
address why people who understand the system perfectly still never start one. The barrier isn't
comprehension, it's the spreads: the hand-lettered, washi-taped pages that make a planning method
look like an art project you're already failing at. This post names that barrier in the first
paragraph, quotes the community's own verdict on it (elaborate spreads are "art about planning",
not the planning system), and then hands the reader a setup they can genuinely finish today with
two objects they already own. It also does two things no page-1 competitor does: it says plainly
what the bullet journal is *bad* at, and it connects the method to weekly planning and time
blocking rather than treating it as a standalone hobby.

## Facts / data to feature prominently

- The official Bullet Journal site's own words: "you do not need to be an artist or an
  organization guru… All you need is an idea of what you want to accomplish, where you are right
  now, a notebook and a pen." The people who invented the method say two objects are enough.
  (Quoted and attributed in prose; the URL is not linked.)
- The community's own verdict, quotable and sharp: elaborate spreads are "art about planning
  rather than… a functional planning system" (u/reallybiglizard, r/bulletjournal, linkable).
- The honest limitation nobody on page 1 mentions: a bullet journal is poor as a live calendar.
  "What the journal can't do is issue reminders - it's a journal" (u/ptdaisy333), with at least
  five commenters in that thread describing the same paper-plus-digital-calendar hybrid.
- The complexity trap, in a real person's words: "I tried to keep a TN alongside my bujo and it
  kind of killed my bujo habit because I had too many ways to process info."
- The rapid-logging symbol set and the four core collections, described in our own words as the
  entire vocabulary a beginner needs.
- Ryder Carroll's own 4-minute explainer has 15.9M+ views and is 11 years old, and people are
  still googling how to start. Cited as a normal external link to the video itself

## Length target

2,600–3,000 words.

This is a deliberate deviation from `blog-craft.md`'s 3,000–5,000 band for `informational_pillar`,
and the reasoning is on the record for the reviewer to challenge. Three inputs point the same way:
the six deep-fetched competitors average ~2,800 words but the median is ~2,000 (two 5,000-word
outliers pull the mean); this blog's twelve published posts run 2,386–3,487 words, so 2,600–3,000
is house length; and a 5,000-word guide would actively contradict the post's own thesis that the
method is simpler and faster than the internet makes it look. Section budget:
intro 200 + §1 280 + §2 300 + §3 240 + §4 740 + §5 280 + §6 300 + §7 300 + CTA 110 + FAQ 250
= 3,000 exactly, the top of the stated range.

## Tone hooks

- Open on the intimidation, not on the method. The first two sentences should be recognisable to
  someone who has bookmarked a bullet journal video and never opened a notebook.
- Translate every term on first use (rapid logging, migration, signifier, collection, spread,
  threading) per `audience.md` §Reading posture. If a term appears without a plain-language
  gloss, that's a review issue.
- Permission-giving rather than instruction-giving. The Reddit data is full of people who needed
  someone to tell them the plain version was allowed. `voice.md`'s rhythm beats ("Ouch!", "Nope!")
  fit naturally where the post dismisses a rule the internet invented.
- Be genuinely honest in §6. A "what it's bad at" section that concludes "but actually it's great"
  is worse than not having one.
- No hustle framing, no "one trick", no shopping list. The post's credibility rests on telling a
  reader they need less than they were told, so any hint of upsell undercuts it.
