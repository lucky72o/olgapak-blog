## Body sections

### H2 1: `Why your desk doesn't look like the one you saved`

- Covers: the gap between the saved photo and the real desk, and the reframe that carries the whole post — an aesthetic desk is a short list of decisions, not a long list of products. Names the three constraints the post writes for: a small desk, possibly in a bedroom, possibly rented, on a real budget.
- Key fact to cite: the top-voted answer people actually give each other is "Forget the lights, tidy all the workspace and then work from there. It will make a huge difference" (`facts.md` §Quotes, u/crusty1uk), and that decluttering out-ranked every product suggestion in 3 of 5 analysed threads (`facts.md` §Named examples — community-sourced fixes).
- Also acknowledge the budget objection head-on here, in the reader's words: "there's no such thing as 'budget' in this economy" (`facts.md` §Quotes, @lakshyalark). The answer: three of the seven steps cost nothing.
- No product mention in this section.
- Approximate word count: 230

### H2 2: `Step 1: Take everything off the desk first`

- Covers: clear the surface completely, put it all in a box, and put back only what earns its way. The one move that changes the most and costs nothing. Then, what to do with the things that *do* come back but shouldn't live on the surface.
- Key fact to cite (external link goes here): objects in view compete for neural representation and mutually suppress each other, which is the honest reason a cleared desk feels different — `facts.md` §Quotes, McMains & Kastner, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3072218/
- Second fact: OSHA's monitor guidance — top of the screen at or below eye level, at least 20 inches away (`facts.md` §Statistics). This is where the riser earns its place: it lifts the screen *and* swallows the small clutter.
- Product (ONE primary pick per the plan's multi-product rule): the **Bamboo Monitor Stand Riser with Drawer**, https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F8VN5T7K?tag=op01e-20 — reason it earns its place: it is the only pick in the post that solves two of the seven decisions at once.
- Named without a link: the **IKEA ALEX drawer unit**, framed as the fix Reddit commenters keep giving each other (`facts.md` §Named examples). Say plainly it is named because readers recommend it, not because it is linked.
- No prices anywhere.
- Approximate word count: 250

### H2 3: `Step 2: Get the cables out of sight`

- Covers: the single most-repeated fix in the research, and the one that most separates a photographed desk from a real one. Route power to one place, get the strip off the floor, and stop the cables crossing the surface. Renter-safe: nothing here requires drilling.
- Key fact to cite: cable management appeared in 3 of 5 analysed Reddit threads, always as one of the first two suggestions offered (`facts.md` §Named examples — community-sourced fixes), and in a reader's own words, "Keep those ugly ass cords out of sight" (`facts.md` §Quotes, u/gin_martini5 — clean up the phrasing when quoting, or paraphrase and attribute).
- Product (ONE primary pick): the **Univivi No Drill Under Desk Cable Management Tray**, https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DK13ZHNJ?tag=op01e-20 — reason it earns its place: no-drill clamp mount, so it works on a rented desk. Brief aside in the same paragraph, not a parallel recommendation: if the desk has no lip to clamp to, a **D-Line Cable Management Box** (https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00846E7JW?tag=op01e-20) hides the strip on the floor instead.
- Approximate word count: 240

### H2 4: `Step 3: Fix the light before you buy any decor`

- Covers: why the ceiling light is what makes a desk look flat in photos and feel wrong at 9pm, and the two-source fix (one warm light at desk level plus whatever daylight there is). Where to put the screen relative to the window. Warm for evening, cooler for daytime work.
- Key fact to cite: OSHA's guidance to place the monitor perpendicular to a window to cut overhead glare (`facts.md` §Statistics) — reference the same OSHA source already linked in Step 1; do NOT emit a second link to the same URL.
- Honest limit: the writer may say, from experience, that constantly raising screen brightness is a sign the room is too dark — but must NOT present it as a rule or a statistic (`facts.md` §Rejected). If the writer wants a sourced claim about light and alertness, emit `[EXTERNAL_LINK_NEEDED: warm vs. cool light and evening alertness, suggested source type: NLM/PMC study or .gov health guidance]` and let Stage 3d resolve it.
- Product (ONE primary pick): the **Honeywell LED Desk Lamp**, https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DS41FKHX?tag=op01e-20 — reason it earns its place: dimmable, so one lamp covers both the working and the evening setting.
- Approximate word count: 250

### H2 5: `Step 4: Pick one surface and one palette`

- Covers: the cheapest way to make mismatched objects look deliberate — put them on one large surface, and hold the accent colour to two or three items. Names the felt-versus-leather choice as a palette decision, not two products. Then the pen problem: every loose pen gets one home.
- Key fact to cite: the analysed competitors' own colour rule, that the accent colour should stay to 2–3 accessories (`research/serp.md` §Selected results analyzed #7) — treat as a styling convention, attribute it as "the rule the design write-ups keep repeating", and do NOT link the competitor.
- Product (ONE primary pick): the **Nordik Felt Desk Mat**, https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CQBVFSS8?tag=op01e-20 — reason it earns its place: one large mat unifies keyboard, mouse and notebook into a single visual block. Brief aside in the same paragraph: for a darker, warmer palette, the **Aothia Leather Desk Pad** (https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08GKBFN99?tag=op01e-20) is the same decision in a different material.
- Second product (the pen decision, its own short paragraph): the **Black Walnut Wooden Pen and Pencil Box**, https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BK5BQ8KD?tag=op01e-20.
- Internal link: `the pens that earn their place` → `/best-pens-for-note-taking`
- Approximate word count: 250

### H2 6: `Step 5: Add one living thing and one thing that's yours`

- Covers: the two additions that stop a tidy desk reading as a showroom. One plant (real if the light allows, fake if it doesn't, and say so without embarrassment), and one or two objects that are actually the reader's. Show the genuine disagreement about personal items rather than pretending there is a rule.
- Key fact to cite (external link goes here): the field study of indoor plants across nine Dutch organisations found the workspace was rated more attractive and people were more satisfied with it, with no measurable effect on 13 of its 18 outcomes — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10346441/ (`facts.md` §Named examples).
- **The honesty beat, and the most differentiating paragraph in the post:** the widely repeated "plants improve productivity by 47%" figure is not in the study it gets attributed to. Say so, say what the study actually found, and land on the better reason — a plant changes how the desk feels, which is the whole point of this post. Never repeat the 47% number as fact (`facts.md` §Rejected).
- Second fact: the personal-items disagreement, quoted from both sides — "Remove figurines lol" against "stick to what you love to maintain character in your space" (`facts.md` §Named examples). Land on one or two things, kept deliberately.
- Product (ONE primary pick): **Mkono Fake Plants in Ceramic Pot**, https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08DG3SHFD?tag=op01e-20 — reason it earns its place: this post's reader often has a desk in a corner with no usable light, and a fake plant that admits it is fake beats a dead real one.
- Internal link: `a notebook worth leaving out` → `/best-notebooks-for-note-taking`
- Approximate word count: 250

### H2 7: `Step 6: Give your phone somewhere else to live`

- Covers: the decision no competing article makes — the desk's biggest visual and attentional problem is usually a phone lying face-up on it. Give it a specific home that is not the desk surface, and preferably not the room.
- **No fact, stat or quote exists in `facts.md` for this section, deliberately.** It runs on reasoned advice and the two internal links only. Do NOT invent a phone-usage, screen-time or attention-span statistic. If a stat is genuinely wanted, mark it `[VERIFY: <claim> | source: <where>]` per blog-craft.md and let Stage 3d resolve or delete it.
- Connects back to Step 1: the riser's drawer from Step 1 is a legitimate phone home; so is another room.
- Internal link: `stop doomscrolling` → `/how-to-stop-doomscrolling`
- No new product.
- Approximate word count: 200

### H2 8: `Step 7: Build the five-minute reset that keeps it this way`

- Covers: the step every competitor skips. Setting the desk up once is easy; the research says keeping it is the hard part. A short end-of-day reset, tied to something that already happens, and permission for the desk to be messy while work is actually happening.
- Key fact to cite: readers say this themselves — "It's always a matter of time before my desk takes its original form as a catch all everytime I reorganize" (`facts.md` §Quotes, @TwiztidShot).
- Internal link: `time blocking` → `/time-blocking`, in the context of ending a work block with the reset.
- No new product.
- Approximate word count: 200

### H2 9: `If you only buy three things`

- Covers: the short, honest answer for a reader who skimmed and wants the shortlist. Three picks already named above, in priority order, each with the decision it serves. Nothing new is introduced here.
- Ordering and rationale: (1) the cable tray, because hiding cables changes the most for the least; (2) the desk mat, because one surface makes everything else look deliberate; (3) the lamp, because the ceiling light is what makes a desk look flat.
- Key fact anchor: restates the community consensus already cited in H2 1 and H2 3 (declutter and cables first) — no new source needed, and no new link. Re-use the three product links already introduced above.
- **Do not turn this into a comparison table or a buying guide.** It is a three-sentence shortlist with the reasoning attached; the post is a how-to, not a roundup.
- Approximate word count: 200

## FAQ block

- Q: `How do I make my desk look aesthetic on a budget?` → A direction: name the three steps that cost nothing (clearing the surface, moving the phone, the reset habit) and say the paid part starts with cables, not decor.
- Q: `What should I actually keep on my desk?` → A direction: only what you use daily plus one plant and one or two personal objects; everything else goes in a drawer, which is what the riser and the ALEX are for.
- Q: `What is a minimalist desk setup?` → A direction: define it plainly as a cleared surface with a single palette, and separate it from "empty" — a minimalist desk still has the things you use.
- Q: `Do plants on a desk really make you more productive?` → A direction: the honest answer, in one or two sentences — the widely quoted 47% figure is not in the study it is attributed to; the study found a workspace people rated more attractive and were more satisfied with, and no effect on most measured outcomes. Cite the same PMC study already linked in H2 6 (no second link).
- Q: `How do I stop my desk getting messy again?` → A direction: the five-minute reset from Step 7, attached to an existing end-of-day trigger, and permission for the desk to be untidy while work is in progress.
