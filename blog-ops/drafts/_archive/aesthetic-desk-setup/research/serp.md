## Search intent

- Dominant intent: informational-visual with a strong commercial tail. The live SERP is not serving a buying-guide comparison; it is serving inspiration/idea galleries first, with "where to shop" or affiliate-linked product lists appended at the end. This confirms the brief's own instruction to record the observed intent rather than echo the content-plan's "COMMERCIAL guide" label.
- Evidence: of the 7 deep-fetched articles, 3 are explicit numbered idea listicles (20/10/8 ideas), 1 opens with a 5-item "basic formula" (stylebyemilyhenderson), 2 are first-person desk tours/series posts naming their own products, and 1 is a long-form design essay with an FAQ block answering "How do I make my desk look aesthetic?". Beyond the analyzed set, `_serp.json.serpFeatures` shows `pinterest_dominant` (3 of the top 19 captured URLs are raw Pinterest boards), plus an image pack, a video pack (TikTok/YouTube), and shopping ads, but no AI Overview and no featured snippet. `peopleAlsoAsk` leads with "How do I make my desk look aesthetic?" (a method question) before "What are some aesthetic things I can put on my desk?" (a shopping question), matching the ideas-then-products pattern in the article bodies themselves.
- Secondary intent: commercial/transactional, present as an appended layer (shop-the-look sections, Amazon/rstyle/Etsy affiliate links, an Amazon search result at rank 18, shopping ads) rather than as the SERP's leading format.
- Implication for our post structure: lead with the small number of styling decisions (surface, light, cables, one personal item) the way stylebyemilyhenderson's "basic formula" does, then attach specific buyable picks per decision, rather than opening with a shopping list the way the SERP's shopping ads and Amazon result do. The PAA's "How do I make my desk look aesthetic?" is a strong H2/FAQ candidate; "What is a minimalist desk setup?" and "What are the latest desk decor trends?" are additional FAQ candidates.

## Audience inferences

- Primary audience: budget-conscious students and early-career professionals working from a small or shared space, matching the brief's stated primary audience. Evidence: savespendsplurge explicitly segments "students, or gamers" as one desk-vibe group and flags Amazon-only access for readers outside big cities; slouchonline and nikkilo both write for renters/single-room setups (nikkilo: "my desk actually came with the place I'm renting").
- Secondary audience: knowledge workers/WFH professionals wanting concrete picks over a gallery, matching stylebyemilyhenderson's and savespendsplurge's "here's exactly what to buy" framing over pure mood-board content.
- Reader knowledge level: beginner. Every analyzed article assumes zero design background and opens with permission-giving language ("you're not alone," "you only need 5 items or less").
- Reader intent: informational leaning commercial (see Search intent section above).

## Angle opportunities

- No analyzed competitor combines a genuine "decisions, not a shopping list" framework (closest is stylebyemilyhenderson's 5-item formula) with real budget/small-room/rental constraints (closest is savespendsplurge and nikkilo) and named, specific, currently-buyable products with prices. This blog can be the first to do all three at once.
- None of the 7 articles connects the aesthetic desk explicitly to phone/distraction management, despite this being a named brief priority and despite Reddit/X data (see `research/reddit.md`, `research/x.md`) showing readers repeatedly cite "declutter first" as the actual fix before any purchase.
- Several articles (aestheticroomcore, octet.design) link to generic Amazon category-search pages or unnamed "Buy on Amazon" anchors instead of naming the actual product; this blog can win on specificity and real named brand+model picks per the brief's explicit requirement.
- orderofficefurniture is the only competitor citing a real study (NLM, plants/productivity), and even that is a single unexplored data point; this blog can build a more credible, better-sourced "why this actually helps focus" section than any competitor currently has.

## Avoid pitfalls

- The "20/10/8 numbered ideas listicle organized by named aesthetic sub-styles (Dark Academia, Kawaii, Cottagecore, etc.)" format is already commoditized across three of the seven analyzed results; repeating it without a stronger organizing principle would blend in.
- Ending every section on an own-brand purchase CTA (Slouch, Order Office Furniture) reads as sales copy; this blog's affiliate links should stay tied to the reader's actual decision, not to a single vendor's catalog.
- Generic "Buy on Amazon" anchor text with no product name in the visible copy (nikkilo, aestheticroomcore) undercuts trust and is explicitly against this blog's brief; every recommended product must be named.
