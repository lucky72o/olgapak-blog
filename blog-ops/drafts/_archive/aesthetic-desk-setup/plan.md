## Angle

Every result on this SERP hands you a gallery and a shopping list; none of them tells you what to decide. This post argues that an aesthetic desk is seven small decisions — what stays on the surface, where the cables go, where the light comes from, one palette, one living thing, one thing that's actually yours, and where the phone lives — and that the first three cost nothing, which is exactly what the Reddit threads say when real people answer this question ("Forget the lights, tidy all the workspace and then work from there"). Products come attached to the decisions they serve, named and linked, never as a list to work through. And it is honest where the competition isn't: the "houseplants boost productivity 47%" line that one ranking article cites is not in the study it points at, and this post says so while citing what that study actually found.

## Facts / data to feature prominently

From `facts.md`. The spine of the post.

- The visual-competition finding: things in view compete for neural representation and mutually suppress each other. This is the post's justification for Step 1, and it replaces the vague "clutter is stressful" line every competitor uses.
- Reddit's actual consensus: decluttering and cable management out-rank every product suggestion, top-voted in 3 of 5 analysed threads. This is the post's structural argument, sourced.
- The 47% plants claim is not in the study it cites — and what that study *did* find (a workspace that people rated more attractive and were more satisfied with) is a better, more honest reason to put a plant on a desk.
- OSHA's three monitor rules, which nothing in the SERP mentions and which double as styling advice (a screen perpendicular to the window looks better *and* has less glare).
- The maintenance problem, in readers' own words: "It's always a matter of time before my desk takes its original form as a catch all everytime I reorganize." This earns Step 7, which no competitor has.
- **Step 6 ("Give your phone somewhere else to live") has no dedicated fact, stat, or quote in
  `facts.md`.** It runs on reasoned advice plus the `how-to-stop-doomscrolling` and
  `how-to-reduce-screen-time` internal links only. Do not invent a phone-usage or attention-span
  statistic to support it; if a stat is wanted there, it goes in as `[VERIFY:]` per
  `blog-craft.md`, never as an asserted fact.

## Tone hooks

- Open on the Pinterest-then-sigh gap in the reader's own words; the highest-value X post in the research set is literally a person saying "I'm so envious of all of you with a cute cool aesthetic desk setup!!!"
- Take the budget objection seriously rather than waving it away — one reply in the research reads "there's no such thing as 'budget' in this economy". The answer is that three of the seven steps cost nothing, and the rest are named items the reader can price for themselves.
- Refuse the "remove everything" orthodoxy. Reddit genuinely disagrees about personal items, and this post lands on "one or two things that are yours" with the disagreement shown, not hidden.
- Be openly skeptical of the 47% stat. That is this blog's voice (tested, not theorized) and it is the single most differentiating paragraph available.
- No dream-office framing. Every step must work on a desk in a bedroom, in a rented room, with no permission to drill anything.
