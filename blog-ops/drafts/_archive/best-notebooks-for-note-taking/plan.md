## Angle

Every competitor either buries you in Japanese-enthusiast paper darlings (JetPens, Gentleman Stationer) or organizes by format instead of by the reader (Wirecutter) — and nobody puts paper picks, reusable options, and a plain-English "what actually makes paper good" primer in one honest guide aimed at a broke, overwhelmed student or new professional. This post fixes that: warm and first-person, it names the trade-offs competitors soft-pedal (Moleskine's thin paper ghosts with fountain pens — with the rarely-published country-of-origin tip; Rocketbook needs the app + FriXion pens), legitimizes the pragmatic "two-notebook" reality (a cheap block for disposable work notes, one nice keeper), and helps the reader self-select a pick by use case in under a minute — without spending $30 to do it.

## Facts / data to feature prominently

- The 60-second buying criteria: **80–100 gsm** paper, **bleed vs ghosting vs feathering**, **~7 mm college ruling**, binding/lay-flat, size, price tier (facts.md).
- Moleskine handled honestly: best with pencil/ballpoint/gel, ghosts with fountain pens — PLUS the country-of-origin tip (look for Germany/Vietnam). (facts.md / reddit.md)
- The "two-notebook" reality: a cheap block for disposable work notes + one nice keeper — you don't have to spend $30. (reddit.md)
- Rocketbook mechanic: reusable, FriXion pen + app scan + damp-cloth wipe; honest paper-vs-digital trade-off. (facts.md)
- Upgrade economics: a good notebook costs only a couple cents/page more than a cheap one. (facts.md, frame as tier)
