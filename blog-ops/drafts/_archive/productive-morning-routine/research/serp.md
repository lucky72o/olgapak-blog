## Audience inferences

Based on the shape of the SERP and the angle competitors take:

- Primary audience: students, early-career professionals and aspiring creators who feel stretched thin (per brief), because the SERP includes a student blog at #5 and two "not a morning person" authors (`05-port-ac-uk.json`, `07-apartmenttherapy-com.json`), signalling searchers who struggle with mornings rather than optimisers.
- Secondary audience: working adults / office workers looking for focus and wellness habits (Asana's "get to work" framing, Real Simple's "before you even open your laptop"), plus busy parents (Camille Styles).
- Reader knowledge level: beginner
- Reader intent: informational

## Angle opportunities

- A method, not a menu: only The Good Space (dated, affiliate-heavy, spiritual framing) offers a build process. A clean step-by-step "design, test for 1 to 2 weeks, adjust" guide with a worksheet-style structure has no strong modern competitor.
- Built for non-morning people and irregular schedules (students, shift-like class timetables): two results are written by self-described night owls but neither offers adaptations for late starts or variable days.
- Short routine plus one protected focus block: Real Simple's expert "non-negotiable focus block" and Camille's "three most important tasks" point at it, but no result connects a short routine to a timeboxed first work block. This fits Olga's timeboxing anecdote and internal links (what-is-timeboxing, time-blocking).
- A minimum-viable / bad-day version of the routine: Camille ("give yourself some grace") and The Good Space ("When Life Happens") admit bad days but give no concrete fallback.
- Phone-first mornings as a dedicated step with concrete tactics (only Real Simple covers it well) and internal links to things-to-do-instead-of-being-on-your-phone / how-to-stop-doomscrolling.
- Sourced claims: every competitor's physiology claims (sleep, light, cortisol, decision fatigue) are either unsourced or routed through blogs/TikTok. Linking primary sources is an easy credibility edge.

## Avoid pitfalls

- 20+ item habit dumps (Asana's 22) with no prioritisation: the long-list format is commoditized and we cannot out-list Asana.
- Celebrity / influencer routines (Mel Robbins, Tim Ferriss, Camille's product-stocked hour): brief asks for a guide, not celebrity 5am routines.
- Affirmation / high-five / "manifestation" content and unsourced health claims (Robbins' TikTok claims, "10 minutes a day ... rewire your brain").
- Product-stuffed routines (supplements, wearables, planners via affiliate links).
- Office-commute assumptions ("Give yourself enough time to get to work") that exclude students.
