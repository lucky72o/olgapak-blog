## What the human wants to convey

- The content-plan angle: a **Time Blocking 101** guide that explains what time blocking actually is
  and then hands the reader a routine they can run on tomorrow's calendar.
- Time blocking is not about grinding harder — it's deciding in advance where your energy goes, so
  the day runs on one decision made while fresh instead of 30 decisions made while tired (per
  `blog.md` §Point of view).
- **Disambiguate time blocking, timeboxing, task batching, and day theming.** These get used
  interchangeably everywhere and the reader genuinely can't tell them apart. This blog already owns
  `/what-is-timeboxing`, so this post must draw the line clearly and link across rather than
  re-explaining timeboxing from scratch (cannibalization risk — see "Other notes").
- Practical and tested, not theorized: a realistic first-week setup cost, concrete block lengths,
  and what a filled-in day actually looks like.
- Cover the failure mode most time-blocking posts skip: **the plan collapses by 11am.** Over-packed
  calendars with zero buffer, blocks sized by wishful thinking, and no rule for what to do when a
  block is blown. A guide that assumes a perfect day is useless by Tuesday.
- Be honest about who time blocking does *not* suit — reactive/interrupt-driven roles and
  unpredictable schedules need a lighter variant, not the full grid.
