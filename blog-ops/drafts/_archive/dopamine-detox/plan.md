## Title candidates (3 options)

1. `Dopamine Detox: What It Is and How to Do It Right`, 49 chars, tone: plainest possible match to the content-plan angle and to what the searcher typed; promises both halves and over-promises neither.
2. `Dopamine Detox: The Honest Guide to Doing It Right`, 50 chars, tone: leads with the differentiator (honesty) rather than the content; risks sounding like it is picking a fight with other guides, and "honest" is the sort of word every guide claims.
3. `Dopamine Detox: Why It Works Even If the Name Doesn't`, 53 chars, tone: the sharpest hook and the closest to the r/Neuropsychology framing ("why does it help if it's bullshit"); the tradeoff is that it hides the how-to half, which is the load-bearing part of the post.

## Recommended title

`Dopamine Detox: What It Is and How to Actually Do It Right`

(58 chars, inside blog-craft.md's strict 50 to 60 range. Option 1 as originally written was 49 chars and failed that rule; "Actually" also does real work here, signalling to a reader who has seen the debunk videos that the post has a method and not just a correction.)

Rationale: option 3 is the better hook but the worse title, because a reader scanning results for a method cannot see that this post has one; option 1 promises exactly the two halves the SERP shows searchers want, and the option-3 hook is not lost, it moves into the H1-adjacent intro and the meta description.

## Angle

Every other result on this page picks a side: the medical authorities correct the name and stop short of a real method, and the how-to guides hand you a protocol without ever admitting the premise is shaky. This post does both, in that order, for a reader who has already watched a debunk video and still wants to know what to actually do on Saturday. It says plainly that you cannot detox from dopamine (Harvard Health is unhedged on this), explains the real reason the practice helps anyway, which is behavioural and has more to do with boredom than with brain chemistry, and then hands over a graduated week that looks more like the cadence the psychologist who coined the term actually proposed than like the 24-hour wall-staring version the internet turned it into.

**The 7-day structure is this post's own on-ramp**, inspired by Sepah's moderate cadence and by the gradual-tapering shape of the r/productivity account (facts.md §Rejected, r/productivity entry); frame it as such, never as Sepah's own protocol. Every specific number inside section 4 must trace to facts.md.

**Founder anecdote:** weave the timeboxing and Parkinson's Law beat into section 4 or 5, tied to the `time-blocking` internal link, per brief.md §Founder anecdote / story. Do not invent a detox-specific story that did not happen.

## Facts / data to feature prominently

From facts.md. These five are the spine:

- Dopamine does not decrease when you avoid overstimulating activities (Harvard Health). This is the correction the whole first half rests on.
- Sepah's own admission that the name is a catchy title, "not to be taken literally" (NYT via Harvard Health).
- Sepah's actual graduated schedule: 1 to 4 hours at the end of a workday, one weekend day per week, one weekend per quarter, one week per year. This is the contrast that makes the viral version look as extreme as it is.
- Harvard Health's documented list of what the viral distortion looks like in practice: not eating, not exercising, no music, no socialising, no talking more than necessary.
- The default mode network becomes more active when you are idle. This is the honest replacement for "resetting your receptors."

## Tone hooks

- **Level with the reader in the first screen.** A meaningful share of this audience arrives having already watched a debunk video (research/serp.md §Search intent). Open by naming that, not by pretending to break the news.
- **Never make a reader who tried this feel stupid.** The r/Productivitycafe quote ("I sat there for 20 minutes before I realized I was just thinking about TikTok. Feels like a scam idk") is a person being honest, not a punchline. Same for the wall-staring account.
- **Name the register the reader has already been marinated in.** research/x.md's corpus finding is that this topic's default internet voice is hustle-culture listicle content ("7 steps to reset your dopamine"). Distancing the post from that voice early is a real differentiator, done without sneering.
- **Boredom, not brain chemistry, is the honest explanation.** Every time the draft reaches for a mechanism, it should reach for behaviour and attention, not for receptors.
- **No fake precision.** This SERP contains no percentages and no timelines, and facts.md rejects four confident numeric or mechanistic claims that circulate widely. If the draft wants a number that is not in facts.md, the answer is to cut the sentence.
- Use Olga's rhythm beats sparingly and where they earn it (voice.md §Lexicon: "Ouch!", "Nope!", rhetorical openers), not as decoration in every section.
