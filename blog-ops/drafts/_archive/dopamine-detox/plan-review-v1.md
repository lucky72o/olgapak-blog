## Reviewer's ruling on the three flagged open questions

1. **Length target (2,200 words).** Keep it. The plan's own math is correct and blog-craft.md's §Body rules example explicitly endorses targeting somewhat above the SERP average over the post-type matrix ceiling when the topic's own evidence base is thin, which facts.md confirms this one is. Do not expand to 3,000-5,000; that would force padding on a topic where the SERP itself reports no stats, no timelines, and a rejected-claims list longer than the verified one.

2. **Section 6's ADHD caveat.** Keep the section; the framing as planned ("this is not one-size-fits-all... that is a conversation for a professional, not a protocol") is safe and is exactly what research/reddit.md's own §Angle opportunities recommends as the responsible way to cover this gap. The one thing missing: the plan should explicitly bar section 6 from citing facts.md's named-example fact that "low dopamine is associated with Parkinson's disease, depression, restless leg syndrome, and ADHD." Placed next to a reader's own ADHD, that association reads as an implied clinical/causal claim about the reader's own brain chemistry, which brief.md's avoid-list bans. The fix is one added sentence to the plan, not a rewrite of the section.

3. **Sepah's LinkedIn article as `primary_source`.** Keep the link. It is not one of the 8 analyzed top-10 SERP URLs in research/serp.md, so the forbidden-competitor rule never applies to it in the first place, and the linkedin.com business/learning-docs allowlist carve-out is the wrong test to worry about here since `primary_source` is a domain-independent classification under blog-craft.md's forbidden-links step 3 (when a competitor cites a claim, route to the document they cite instead of the competitor). Both Cleveland Clinic and Harvard Health, the two most authoritative pages in this SERP, link straight to this same document for the same claim, which is exactly the scenario that rule anticipates. The plan's own open-question wording conflates the two tests; it should be corrected, not the underlying decision.
