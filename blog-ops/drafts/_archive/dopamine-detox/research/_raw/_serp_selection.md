# SERP selection: dopamine detox

Observed intent: **informational, with a strong "is this actually real?" verification sub-intent.** The live top 10 is not a how-to SERP — it is dominated by medical authorities (Cleveland Clinic, Harvard Health, MedicalNewsToday, a PMC literature review, Crisis Text Line) explaining that "dopamine detox" is a misnamed pop-science trend, interleaved with practical guides that teach the protocol anyway. Google surfaced `people_also_ask` and no AI overview at capture time. The searcher wants a definition first, a verdict on whether it works second, and a doable method third — in that order.

Reviewed top 8 results from `_serp.json`. Selected 7 (target 5–8, hard cap 8) for deep fetch:

1. (rank 2) https://health.clevelandclinic.org/dopamine-detox , the highest-ranking direct competitor and the clearest statement of the dominant frame ("detoxes don't work, here's what to do instead"); its structure is what this post has to beat.
2. (rank 3) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11223451/ , a peer-reviewed literature review on dopamine and holistic well-being; primary-source class, and the only academic material in the top 10.
3. (rank 4) https://biogena.com/en-gb/knowledge/blog/dopamine-detox-guide , the pro-protocol camp ("benefits, rules & how to get started"); needed to see what the "how to do it" half of the SERP actually teaches, even though the publisher sells supplements.
4. (rank 5) https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/dopamine-detox , the mainstream middle-ground explainer; likely source of the definitions and the Cameron Sepah protocol framing that the rest of the SERP echoes.
5. (rank 6) https://www.reddit.com/r/productivity/comments/1i2wqp1/this_30day_dopamine_detox_reset_my_brain_and/ , a lived-experience thread ranking organically in the top 10; shows the language real people use about their own attempt, and what they say actually changed.
6. (rank 7) https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/dopamine-fasting-misunderstanding-science-spawns-a-maladaptive-fad-2020022618917 , Harvard Health's debunk; authoritative-allowlist class and the strongest citable source for the "the name is wrong" argument the brief wants in the first screen.
7. (rank 8) https://www.crisistextline.org/blog/2026/03/26/dopamine-detox/ , the most recent piece in the set (2026) and a mental-health organisation's framing; useful for the "when this is not the right tool, talk to someone" boundary the brief requires.

Skipped:
- rank 1 (https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/addiction-support/drug-addiction-getting-help/) , off-topic: an NHS page about getting help for substance addiction, surfaced by the word "detox" rather than by the query's actual meaning. Nothing in it addresses the practice this post is about.
