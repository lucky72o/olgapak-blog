**Corpus-level finding (from `_x_selection.md`, confirmed by the 5 fetched posts):** X's dopamine-detox discourse is overwhelmingly hustle-culture, self-improvement listicle content ("7 steps to detox your dopamine levels and reset your mind," "9 lessons from the book Dopamine Detox," near-identical cross-posted listicles at multiple ranks). This register is explicitly banned by the brief's avoid-list ("no hustle-culture framing," "no 'one trick rewires your brain' overclaiming") and by `blog-ops/profile/audience.md`. The 5 posts below were selected for perspective spread, not for representativeness, and none should be used as a voice model for this blog's post. The corpus shape itself, that the internet's default framing of this topic is hustle-culture, is a fact worth naming in the post's opening, since the target reader has almost certainly already seen this exact register.

## Industry-take themes

- **Theme: The extreme "work, read, stare at a wall" version is real and currently circulating (attributed to "Alex Becker's" approach), and reactions to it are mixed between admiration and explicit pushback that it's too limiting.**
  - Evidence: "All I do is: Work / Read / Stare at a wall", @liamottley_, source: https://x.com/liamottley_/status/2094070276031914357
  - Evidence: "yeah, just makes life feel so limited... diversity... make up the best days ive ever lived", @itsjulianhoward, same URL
- **Theme: A moderate reframe ("micro-detox" via ordinary low-stimulation activities like walking) draws more warmth and less argument than the strict listicle-protocol posts.**
  - Evidence: "Going on a walk is a micro dopamine detox.", @thedankoe, source: https://x.com/thedankoe/status/1857074770057089172 (202 replies per `_x_selection.md`, overwhelmingly agreement, no counter-take found among the visible replies in this fetch)
- **Theme: A recurring counter-take reframes the entire practice as "don't restrict dopamine, redirect it toward productive activities" rather than avoiding stimulation altogether.**
  - Evidence: "it's never about restricting it, but controlling the activities it comes from", @eptwts, source: https://x.com/eptwts/status/1954260984266211460
  - Evidence: "it was never about cut off yourself from everything.. it was about replacing screen time for a book reading or porn for time in nature.", @palcevskij, same URL
- **Theme: The term is understood on X partly as a commercial/cultural signal (a "flex," a market opportunity) rather than a personal health practice.**
  - Evidence: "Someone will make $1B in the 'digital detox' space... being more dopamine sober than dopamine drunk will be a modern day flex.", @gregisenberg, source: https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/1736058260908572793

## Counterpoints / contrarian takes

- Position: the "detox" framing itself is meaningless because you can't meaningfully restrict dopamine, only redirect what triggers it: "the detox part is a scam, it does nothing", @eptwts, source: https://x.com/eptwts/status/1954260984266211460
- Position: the extreme version is too restrictive to be sustainable or desirable: "yeah, just makes life feel so limited. pulses of that are good, but diversity... make up the best days ive ever lived.", @itsjulianhoward, source: https://x.com/liamottley_/status/2094070276031914357

## Angle opportunities for this blog

- Open by naming the hustle-culture register directly (per `_x_selection.md`'s corpus-level finding): most readers coming from social media have already seen "7 steps to reset your dopamine" content; explicitly distancing this post from that register, without mocking readers who tried it, would be a genuine differentiation X's corpus does not offer anywhere in this sample.
- The @eptwts / @palcevskij exchange offers a clean, non-jargon reframe already in plain reader language ("controlling the activities it comes from" / "replacing screen time for a book... or time in nature") that maps directly onto the brief's "deliberate break from high-stimulation, low-effort dopamine hits" framing, this could be paraphrased (not quoted at length) as a bridge between the debunk and the protocol sections.
- The "stare at a wall" extreme (@liamottley_) is a concrete, real, currently-circulating example of exactly the version the brief wants explicitly separated from the useful one; safe to reference generically ("some versions have people staring at a wall for hours") without naming the specific poster, since the underlying claim ("this exists and people are doing it") is corroborated independently by the brief's own description of the "viral TikTok/YouTube version."
