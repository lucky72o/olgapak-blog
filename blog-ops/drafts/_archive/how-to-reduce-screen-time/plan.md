## Angle

Most screen-time advice tells you to use less phone, which is like telling someone to spend less money without ever looking at the receipt. This guide starts by splitting your hours into the screen time you'd choose again (work, study, calls with people you love) and the screen time that just leaked, then hands you fixes ordered by how much effort they cost — ten-minute settings changes first, structural changes last — so you can stop at whatever tier makes your week feel different. It is also honest about the part every other guide skips: app limits and grayscale get swiped past within a fortnight, and the post says exactly what to do when that happens instead of pretending willpower will hold. The evidence backs the modest version: in a two-week study published in PNAS Nexus, only about a quarter of participants fully complied and 91% still improved on at least one measure of well-being, attention, or mental health.

## Facts / data to feature prominently

From `facts.md`. The spine of the post.

- Only about a quarter of participants fully complied with the two-week reduction study, and 91% still improved on at least one measure of well-being, attention, or mental health. This is the permission structure for the whole post: partial beats perfect.
- Participants roughly halved their screen time, from about five hours a day to about two and a half.
- Participants slept about 20 minutes more per night during the study period.
- NIH/NHLBI guidance: no more than two hours a day of recreational screen time — used as a reference point for the "what should I aim for" section, explicitly not as a rule to feel guilty about.
- Voice of customer, the failure mode: "I've used app blockers that lock apps, but I always found ways around them. I tried switching my phone to grayscale, but that didn't last either." (r/digitalminimalism) — this is what section 5 exists to answer.

## Tone hooks

- **No moralizing, ever.** The reader already knows their number is high. Diagnose, don't scold. Ban any sentence that implies the reader is weak or wasting their life.
- **No absolutism.** "Delete all social media" is not the advice; the digital-detox post owns the intensive-reset territory and this one owns sustainable reduction.
- **Own the messiness.** Say plainly that limits get swiped past and grayscale gets switched back, because every reader has already done both.
- **Translate every specialist term on first use** (dopamine, variable reward, attention residue, doomscrolling) per `audience.md` §Reading posture — or, better, use the plain-language version and skip the term.
- Olga's own anecdote: adopting timeboxing to beat Parkinson's Law (work expands to fill the time you give it), reframed as giving leisure phone use its own slot instead of letting it fill every gap.
- Acknowledge the counter-narrative once ("the hours aren't the point, what you do with them is") without adopting its hustle framing; `voice.md` and `audience.md` both forbid grind-culture language.
