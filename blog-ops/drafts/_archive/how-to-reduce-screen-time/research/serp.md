## Audience inferences

- Primary audience: general adults searching for personal behavior-change tactics — the SERP skews adult-general (Scripps explicitly says "for Adults"), not teen/parenting, which matches brief.md's primary audience of students/early-career professionals.
- Secondary audience: people motivated by health consequences first (Health Shield, harmonyhit both lead with anxiety/sleep/eye-strain stats) — suggests some searchers need the "why" before the "how."
- Reader knowledge level: beginner — every competitor result explains basic concepts (what doomscrolling is, what Screen Time settings are) rather than assuming familiarity.
- Reader intent: informational, and specifically "give me an ordered list of concrete actions" — 5 of 7 results are literally numbered actionable lists.

## Angle opportunities

- No competitor names iOS Screen Time / Android Digital Wellbeing mechanics in real depth — Health Shield just links out to Apple/Google's own docs, Guardian says "built-in tools" without naming them. A pillar post that actually walks through the phone-specific mechanics (per brief.md) is a genuine content gap.
- No competitor addresses "what to do when app limits/timers get dismissed" as its own explicit section — MD Anderson gets closest (motivations-first framing) but doesn't name the workaround problem directly. Reddit data (see reddit.md) shows this is the #1 real-world failure mode users report.
- No competitor draws the brief's "intentional vs. leaked screen time" distinction as a structuring principle — MD Anderson gestures at it (video chat vs. doomscrolling) but doesn't build the whole piece around it. This is a clear differentiation opportunity for the pillar post.
- No competitor orders tactics lowest-friction-to-hardest — all are flat numbered lists with no explicit escalation logic (Guardian gets close by ending on "dumbphone" and "cold turkey" as later items, but doesn't frame it that way explicitly).
- The Georgetown/Kushlev study result (partial detox works, moderation is enough) is underused across the SERP — only Georgetown cites it, and even they frame it as "digital detox." This blog's pillar post can cite the same primary study to support "reduce, don't eliminate" without touching the detox framing at all.

## Avoid pitfalls

- Generic uncited stats ("X% feel anxious without their phone," "average person spends N hours") appear in multiple competitors without sourcing — don't repeat unsourced numbers; use only the Georgetown/Kushlev study and NIH/NCBI/Mayo sources harvested below.
- Parenting/family-screen-time framing (Scripps' "Be a role model" section, listsof30's kid sticker charts, Health Shield's family-oriented tips) is off-audience per brief.md and already commoditized in this SERP — avoid drifting into it.
- Flat, unordered numbered lists (7 tips, 8 tips, 30 micro-habits with no structure) are the default format here — a structured pillar with clear friction-based ordering and a why/what/how hierarchy differentiates rather than blends in.
