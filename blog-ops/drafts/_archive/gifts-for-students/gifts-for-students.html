Start With One Question: Paper or Screen?

Most study gifts split on a single detail: how the student takes notes. A handwriter gets nothing from a stylus, and an iPad note-taker gets very little from a beautiful mechanical pencil.

A commenter in r/EngineeringStudents, answering a relative shopping for an incoming student, put it perfectly: "if he takes notes on an iPad this is moot, but a pack of really nice mechanical pencils would have been nice for me."

So how do you find out without spoiling the surprise?

Look at what they carry to class, or quietly ask a parent, a sibling, or a friend. Then:

  • They write by hand: start with the handwriting picks just below.
  • They use an iPad: skip ahead to the two stylus picks.
  • You can't tell: the focus and planning gifts work either way, and a gift card is a fair fallback (more on that near the end).

Some students already know the phone is the problem. The kSafe is a timed lock box sold for phones: set the dial and it locks for anywhere from one minute to ten days. Does locking a phone away actually help?

Nobody has studied lock boxes that I know of, but researchers have studied the same idea. In a PNAS Nexus study, an app blocked all mobile internet on participants' phones (calls and texts still worked), and blocking mobile internet for two weeks cut screen time from 314 to 161 minutes a day. That study tested an app, not a box. The kSafe is simply a physical version of the same hard stop.

  • Best for: the student who knows the phone is the problem and wants a hard stop
  • Budget: Mid-range
  • Skip it if: they didn't ask for it (as a surprise, a lock box can read like a lecture)

Gifts to Skip (and When a Gift Card Is the Better Gift)

What about the gifts that sound useful and aren't?

Picking well is half the job. The other half is skipping these:

  • Blue light glasses. They sound like the perfect screen-time gift. Nope! The American Academy of Ophthalmology doesn't recommend any special eye wear for computer use, and it notes that "Several studies suggest that blue light-blocking glasses do not improve symptoms of digital eye strain." Give a habit instead: the AAO's 20-20-20 rule, "every 20 minutes, shift your eyes to look at an object at least 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds." The Time Timer above can nudge that break.
  • Expensive pens. They go missing, remember? Buy the multipack and let a few wander off.
  • Gear for the job they're training for. Professional gear can feel thoughtful to you and pointless to them while they're still studying. In a Reddit thread about a student starting nursing, one commenter pleaded: "please don't buy a stethoscope! It's very rarely used during practice."
  • When a gift card wins. If you couldn't answer the paper-or-screen question, a gift card isn't a cop-out. One Reddit thread, where a relative asked a university subreddit what to buy an incoming student, landed on gift cards almost unanimously. The best tip from it: give one bigger card instead of several small ones, because "that's how they end up not having the one they need."

FAQ

What is a good gift for a college student?

A good gift for a college student is something that fits into their daily study routine, not something that looks impressive and then sits in a drawer. Start by finding out how they take notes: handwriters get the most from a good notebook, a multipack of pens, or a mechanical pencil, while iPad note-takers get the most from a stylus that works with their iPad. If you can't find out, focus gifts like the Loop Quiet 2 earplugs or the Time Timer Home MOD work for any student.

What are good cheap gifts for students?

The small-budget picks in this guide are the Uni-ball Jetstream RT 12-pack, the Time Timer Home MOD, the Pentel GraphGear 1000 mechanical pencil, Loop Quiet 2 earplugs, and the Equity 33100 analog alarm clock. Each one solves an everyday problem: lost pens, losing track of time mid-study, math homework, noisy rooms, or a phone that sneaks into bed. Cheap doesn't have to mean throwaway when the gift gets used every day.

Is a gift card a good gift for a student?

Yes, especially when you don't know how they study. If you can't tell whether they write by hand or on an iPad, a gift card lets them choose the gear that fits how they actually work. Give one bigger card rather than several small ones, so they aren't stuck without the one they need.

Are blue light glasses a good gift for students?

No. The American Academy of Ophthalmology does not recommend any special eyewear for computer use, and it notes that several studies suggest blue light-blocking glasses do not improve symptoms of digital eye strain. A better gift is a break habit: the AAO's 20-20-20 rule, where every 20 minutes you look at something at least 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. A visual timer can help them remember it.

What should I get a student who takes notes on an iPad?

An Apple Pencil (USB-C) is the one to get once you've confirmed it works with their iPad, because Apple Pencil compatibility depends on the iPad model. If you can't check without spoiling the surprise, the Logitech Crayon is the safer choice, since its listing says it works with all iPads from 2018 releases and later.
