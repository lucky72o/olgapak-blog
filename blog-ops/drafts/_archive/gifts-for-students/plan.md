## Title candidates (3 options)

1. `Best Gifts for Students: 15 Picks They'll Actually Use`, 54 chars, tone: answers the reader's real fear (the "just give a gift card" objection from Reddit) head-on; practical, warm.
2. `Best Gifts for Students: 15 Useful Ideas for Note-Takers`, 56 chars, tone: leans on the niche that page one leaves empty; narrower, may under-serve givers who don't think of the student as a "note-taker".
3. `Gifts for Students: 15 Best Picks for Study and Focus`, 53 chars, tone: keyword truly first, outcome-led; flatter and less reader-voiced.

## Recommended title

`Best Gifts for Students: 15 Picks They'll Actually Use`

Rationale: it keeps "best" plus the full keyword in the first four words, uses an odd number, and makes the promise the research says givers care about most (a gift that gets used, not one that "may stay in their desk" or goes missing).

## Meta description (draft)

`Stuck on gifts for students? 15 picks for note-taking, focus, and planning that students actually use, sorted by need with a budget band, plus what to skip.`

## Angle

Most "gifts for students" lists are 35 to 50 random items where a mushroom lamp sits next to a laptop, and none of them take study gear seriously. This guide sorts 15 genuinely useful gifts by what they do for a student (take better notes on paper or on a screen, focus in a noisy dorm, shared room, or bedroom, plan and sleep better), tags each with a budget band, and opens with the one question that decides most of it: does the student take notes by hand or on an iPad? It is also honest about what to skip, including blue light glasses (the American Academy of Ophthalmology doesn't recommend them) and when a gift card really is the smarter gift.

## Facts / data to feature prominently

From `facts.md`. Top 3–5 that are the spine of the post.

- AAO: "The Academy does not recommend any special eye wear for computer use." (the honest "skip this" that differentiates the guide)
- The Q20i's own FAQ admits its noise cancelling is weak on voices, which is why earplugs sit next to headphones in the focus section.
- Castelo et al. (PNAS Nexus, 2025): when phones lost mobile internet for two weeks, screen time fell from 314 to 161 minutes a day (context for the kSafe and analog alarm clock, described honestly: the study blocked internet, it did not test a lock box).
- Apple Pencil compatibility is model-specific: check the iPad model before buying.
- Reddit voice: "nice pens (well, any pen!) go missing in a flash" and a whole thread that resolves to "just get a gift card", both addressed directly.

## Tone hooks

Specific tonal notes for this post beyond the default brand voice:

- Talk to the giver as a friend who has been the student: warm, a little funny about gifts that end up "staying in the desk", never preachy.
- Honest over exhaustive: each pick gets a one-line "best for" and, where true, a "skip it if" (e.g. skip the Apple Pencil if you don't know their iPad model).
- No hustle-culture framing; focus gifts are about a calmer study session, not grinding harder.
- Evergreen: works for back-to-school, birthdays, graduation, and the holidays; never "this season".
