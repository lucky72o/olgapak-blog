## Angle

Every list on page one hands you nouns and quietly assumes you will buy a kit: the top result links out to items from £33 to £450, and a 337-comment Reddit thread exists purely because people are tired of being told pottery is a cheap alternative to scrolling when it costs $300 to touch clay. This post does the two things none of them do. It tags every single hobby with an honest cost tier, free through real investment, so nobody gets sold a hobby they cannot afford. And it draws a line the whole genre blurs: a hobby is not the same thing as a ten-minute filler activity, so if you want something to do with an idle Tuesday evening you get sent to the right post instead, and what stays here is the stuff with a learning curve and a next session. Then, because nobody fails at hobbies for lack of ideas, it closes on the part that actually decides whether any of this survives February.

## Tone hooks

- **Not a phone-shaming post.** Readers describe the problem themselves as an empty, compulsive evening, not a moral failure. Match that. `audience.md` §Language to avoid rules out the alarmist register, and the "cultivate a hobby before AI takes your job" framing that is currently circulating on X was deliberately excluded from research for the same reason.
- **Not a hustle post.** A hobby does not need to be monetisable, a side hustle, or productive. Say so, once, plainly. It is on-brand and it is what this audience is tired of.
- **Honest about leakage.** Most analog hobbies pull you back onto a screen eventually (patterns, tutorials, ordering supplies). Acknowledge it rather than pretending purity; the X post that admits "minus the switch" is the right register.
- **Use "analog hobby" once**, as the reader's own vernacular, translated on first use per `audience.md` §Niche jargon translation. It is the live term in organic posts and the rank-5 SERP result uses it as its primary keyword.
- **Show, do not just name.** Both Reddit and X reward specificity: a named route, a real first project, a second-hand source. Every entry should read like someone actually did it.
- Olga's return-to-study story is the anecdote to reach for; it argues the post's thesis (a durable pursuit beats a filler activity) without being a productivity flex.
