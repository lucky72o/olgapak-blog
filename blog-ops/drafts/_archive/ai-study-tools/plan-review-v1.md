## Discussion of the three flagged judgment calls

**1. The "tested" problem.** Dropping "Tested" from the title and stating the actual method plainly in §1 is the right minimum bar: it prevents the post from making a claim it can't back. But it doesn't fully close the gap with `blog.md` §Point of view ("every tool and tactic recommended here is something Olga has personally tested"), which is a blanket promise about every post's *content*, not just its title. A 9-pick roundup built entirely from Reddit consensus plus vendor documentation, with zero hands-on use, is a real departure from that promise, not a wording issue solvable by omitting one word from the H1. I'm not recommending reject: the plan's mitigation is honest, the angle doesn't depend on a testing claim to work, and this blog has a human Gate 2 review before anything goes live per `custom-instructions.md` §Publishing & review. But "flag it and move on" isn't quite enough given how central this promise is to the blog's identity. The plan should add one concrete, actionable step: recommend at Gate 2 that Olga spot-test at least the free/cheapest picks she can check in minutes (NotebookLM, ChatGPT Study Mode, and one paid all-in-one such as Studley, since its free-tier ceiling is the most restrictive and most checkable) before publish, and make explicit that if she doesn't, no sentence in the draft may imply firsthand use.

**2. Forbidden-link vs. affiliate-link collision.** I don't think the precedence rule actually forces linking the five/six top-10 vendor pages. Read in full, `custom-instructions.md`'s "Product links: ship real, working AFFILIATE links now" bullet is scoped to monetizable links: its own text is entirely about Amazon ASIN/tag mechanics and a "non-Amazon... where Amazon doesn't carry the genuine item" fallback that maps to `product.md` §Future monetization direction (physical products via Amazon Associates, not live yet). None of the nine reviewed tools generates any revenue for this blog whether linked or not, so there's no real affiliate-link question in play, and therefore no genuine conflict for precedence to resolve for the specific tools that also happen to rank in this keyword's own top 10. `blog-craft.md`'s forbidden-link rule exists exactly to stop a post from handing outbound link equity, and literal reader traffic, to the pages currently outranking it for its own target keyword, which is precisely what would happen here. I read the plan's name-not-link resolution for Studley/StudyFetch/Turbo AI/Mindgrasp/Ask Maeve/Evernote as correct on the merits, not merely "the conservative route." Where the plan under-delivers is the four reviewed tools that are NOT top-10 URLs (Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Otter.ai): for those there is no SEO downside and no monetization question either way, so custom-instructions.md's "give every product a real link now" applies cleanly, and the plan should commit to linking all four rather than leaving it conditional on the external-link budget (see issue 3).

**3. Evidence quality.** Clean. All five spine facts (§Facts / data to feature prominently) trace to `facts.md`'s ✅ Verified tier: the SERP-shape facts, the two-of-fifteen roundup overlap, the free-tier-transparency gap, NotebookLM's Reddit/roundup vs. vendor-homepage split, and the four-shared-features observation. Nothing from the ⚠️ vendor-self-report tier (the 3M/8M/10M/5M user counts, StudyFetch's 92%/85%/30% internal study) is promoted into the spine; where the plan does reference tier-⚠️ material (the Gallup 57% stat, NotebookLM's Reddit-sourced free-tier numbers) it's correctly gated behind Stage 3d primary-source verification with an explicit "delete if unconfirmed" instruction. The ❌-tier MIT cognitive-debt study is correctly kept out of §7 as a stated finding.
