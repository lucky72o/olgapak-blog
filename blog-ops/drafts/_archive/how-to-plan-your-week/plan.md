## Angle

Most weekly-planning guides tell you to design your *ideal* week — then that perfect plan collapses the first time Tuesday doesn't cooperate. This post takes the opposite stance: a good weekly plan assumes the week *will* go sideways, so it's built around deciding once where your energy goes, timeboxing only the essentials, leaving deliberate slack, and — the part almost every other guide skips — a quick mid-week reset for when the plan has already broken. It's planning as a single upfront decision that saves you 20 tired decisions later, not planning as grinding harder.

## Key sections (preview, becomes the outline in Phase 2)

1. Planning your week isn't about doing more (reframe: deciding in advance where your energy goes; preempt the "is this just productivity theater?" skepticism from the research)
2. Why most weekly plans fall apart (the over-planning trap: "idealism," a plan built for a perfect week; introduce the "rocks and water" mental model)
3. How to plan your week, step by step (the numbered core routine, H3 steps):
   - Step 1: Reflect on last week and do a brain-dump
   - Step 2: Choose your priorities for the week (your "rocks")
   - Step 3: Timebox the essentials — give each task a slot (define timeboxing on first use; link to /what-is-timeboxing)
   - Step 4: Build in slack (protect your "margin")
   - Step 5: Keep one home for the plan (one source of truth, not a new app every week)
4. The mid-week reset: what to do when your plan breaks by Tuesday (the differentiator — its own H2)
5. How much time weekly planning really takes (realistic expectations, ~15–60 min; "simple" doesn't mean effortless — anti-overclaim)
6. When you're truly overloaded, plan less — not more (do-less / rank-don't-balance; speaks to the overloaded-student persona; non-judgmental tone on "nothing sticks")
7. Tools that make weekly planning easier — and your next step (one source of truth + calendar; soft Email Generator mention for the admin block; flows into the CTA)
8. FAQ

- Calm, forgiving, non-judgmental. The research shows "nothing sticks" is widely shared and sometimes neurological (ADHD) — never imply it's a willpower failure.

- Translate jargon on first use: timeboxing, Parkinson's Law, "margin/slack." No unexplained kanban/GTD.
- No prices of any kind.
