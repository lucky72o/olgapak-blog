## Body sections

Every H2 cites ≥1 fact from facts.md. Product mention appears once, in §7 only.

### H2 1: `Planning your week isn't about doing more`

- Covers: reframe the whole premise — planning is deciding in advance where your limited energy goes, not a promise to grind harder. Preempt the reader's skepticism ("is this just productivity theater?") head-on, which the research shows is a real objection.
- Key fact to cite: the "manage your energy, not your time" framing, facts.md Named examples / External, source: https://hbr.org/2007/10/manage-your-energy-not-your-time (authoritative_allowlist — the post's spine external link; Stage 3d verifies it loads + supports the claim).
- Internal link: `the benefits of planning ahead` → /benefits-of-planning-ahead-for-peak-productivity (why planning at all — place EARLY in the section, before the HBR external link, so the first body link is internal).
- Tone: calm, non-judgmental. Reference (paraphrase) the community skepticism "is planning just another form of procrastination / productivity theater?" from research/reddit.md and answer it.
- Approx word count: ~260

### H2 2: `Why most weekly plans fall apart`

- Covers: name the over-planning failure mode explicitly — a plan built for a *perfect* week (no interruptions, steady energy) breaks the first time reality doesn't cooperate. Introduce the "rocks and water" mental model as the fix-in-miniature (schedule fixed commitments; let flexible work flow into the gaps).
- Key fact to cite: "Idealism causes people to make structured weekly plans without considering interruptions, shifting priorities, or energy fluctuations…" — facts.md Quotes (Sheena McGinley / Memtime). Attribute the quote; do NOT link memtime.com (top-10 SERP competitor — quote-and-attribute by name only, no hyperlink).
- Also usable: "rocks and water" (facts.md Named examples, Reddit) — credit as a reader/community framing, paraphrase.
- Approx word count: ~260

### H2 3: `How to plan your week, step by step`

- Covers: the core numbered routine. Short framing paragraph, then five H3 steps. This is the how-to spine the SERP expects.
- Key fact to cite: "I don't plan every minute, I plan for momentum" (facts.md Quotes, Reddit) as the ethos for the whole routine.
- Approx word count: ~640 (across the five sub-steps)

#### H3 3.1: `Step 1 — Reflect on last week and empty your head`

- Weekly reset + brain-dump: what got done, what didn't, what's carrying over; dump every open loop onto one page so it's out of your head.
- Fact/quote: Tara Viswanathan's admission that even a simple structure "takes 45 min to 1 hr… including reflection from last week" (facts.md Quotes) — sets an honest expectation that reflection is real work.

#### H3 3.2: `Step 2 — Choose your priorities (your "rocks")`

- Pick the few things that actually matter this week (2–4), not everything. These are the non-negotiables.
- Internal link: `stay focused on your goals` → /how-to-stay-focused-on-goals (choosing what matters).
- Quote option: "Most people plan their week around tasks. I plan mine around progress" (facts.md Quotes, @CryptoMaliel) as an optional outcome-first lens.

#### H3 3.3: `Step 3 — Give each priority a slot (timeboxing)`

- Timeboxing defined on FIRST use: block specific time on the calendar for each priority so the task has a home, not a vague "someday." Tie to Olga's Parkinson's Law anecdote (work expands to fill the time you give it — define Parkinson's Law in plain words on first use).
- Internal link: `how timeboxing works` → /what-is-timeboxing (closest sibling — the deep-dive).
- External link opportunity: Parkinson's Law origin — mark `[EXTERNAL_LINK_NEEDED: Parkinson's Law originated in a 1955 Economist article by C. Northcote Parkinson, suggested source type: The Economist archive or Encyclopaedia Britannica]` (both allowlist/authoritative; Stage 3d resolves).
- [IMAGE] slot 1 (remotion diagram) goes in this section, after the first paragraph — see Image placement plan.

#### H3 3.4: `Step 4 — Leave slack on purpose`

- Deliberately leave unscheduled buffer — "margin." A week with no empty space has no room to absorb the unexpected, so it snaps. This is where over-planning gets prevented.
- Key quote: "Margin is breathing room. It's your schedule's emergency fund…" (facts.md Quotes, Jessica Massey).
- Internal link: `protect those focus blocks from doomscrolling` → /how-to-stop-doomscrolling (guarding the slots/slack you just carved out).

#### H3 3.5: `Step 5 — Keep one home for the plan`

- One source of truth (a single notebook or app), not a new tool every week. Serial system-hopping is why nothing sticks.
- Key quote: "a messy plan you actually follow is 100x better than a beautiful Notion dashboard you never open" (facts.md Quotes, Reddit).

### H2 4: `The midweek reset: what to do when the plan breaks`

- Covers: THE differentiator. No competitor offers this. When it's Wednesday and the plan is already dead, don't scrap the whole system or wait for next Sunday. Do a 5-minute reset: look at what's left, re-pick the 1–2 things that still matter, re-slot them into the days you have left, and consciously drop the rest without guilt.
- Key fact: reuse the "idealism" gap framing (facts.md Quotes, McGinley) — the reset is the recovery mechanism idealistic plans lack.
- Tone: forgiving. A broken plan is expected, not a personal failing (research shows "nothing sticks" is widely shared, sometimes neurological — never imply willpower failure).
- [IMAGE] slot 2 (ai-prompt scene) goes here, after the first paragraph.
- Approx word count: ~310

### H2 5: `How long weekly planning actually takes`

- Covers: set honest expectations. Somewhere from ~15 minutes (light version) to ~an hour (with real reflection). "Simple" doesn't mean effortless — a simple output still takes real thought. Explicitly rejects the "one trick, five minutes, life transformed" overclaim.
- Key facts/quotes: Tara's "45 min to 1 hr" and the community's short-attempt reality (facts.md Quotes) — a range, not a promise.
- Approx word count: ~200

### H2 6: `When you're truly overloaded, plan less — not more`

- Covers: for the overloaded reader (the student persona), the honest answer isn't a better time-blocking trick — it's cutting scope. Rank your commitments and let the lowest ones run in "maintenance mode." Planning can't manufacture hours.
- Key quotes: "Do less things" and the "rank them, don't balance them" reframe (facts.md Quotes, Reddit).
- Tone: direct but kind; this speaks to pain #1 (overwhelmed).
- Approx word count: ~250

- Covers: keep tooling minimal — a calendar for the rocks, one list for the water, one home for the plan. ## FAQ block

- Q: `How do I plan my week when I don't know what's coming?` → A direction: separate fixed commitments ("rocks") from flexible work ("water") and leave deliberate slack, so surprises land in the gaps instead of breaking the plan.
- Q: `What's the best day to plan my week?` → A direction: Sunday is common, but the specific day matters far less than doing it consistently on the same day each week.
- Q: `How long should planning my week take?` → A direction: roughly 15–60 minutes depending on how much reflection you do; a simple plan still takes real thought, so don't expect it to be effortless.
- Q: `What do I do when my weekly plan falls apart midweek?` → A direction: don't scrap it or wait for next Sunday — run a 5-minute midweek reset: re-pick what still matters, re-slot it into the days left, and drop the rest guilt-free.
- Q: `Is planning every day worth it, or is it just productivity theater?` → A direction: plan the week for direction and do light daily check-ins; a messy plan you actually follow beats a perfect one you abandon.
