## Whether issue #1 is genuinely resolved

The added text under Key sections item 2 reads: *"Three items on this list... have honest trade-offs that describe them as failing this exact filter. The category earns its slot; the pack size does not. Section 8 must say so plainly and tell the reader to buy fewer colors / a smaller pack than the linked product where that option exists, rather than let the contradiction stand unaddressed."*

This is a real resolution strategy, not a restated acknowledgment: it draws a specific, defensible line (category-level pick vs. quantity purchased) and gives the outline writer a concrete instruction for what Section 8 must do with it, which is exactly what was missing in iteration 1. It maps cleanly onto two of the three items: the Sarasa Clip 10-pack (advice: you don't need to rotate all 10 colors) and the Mr. Pen 480-tab pack (advice: 480 tabs will outlast this notebook, that's fine, don't feel obligated to use them all). For the Bande washi roll stickers, the trade-off in `facts.md` is specifically about the item being likely to "sit unused in a drawer," which is a different failure mode than pack size, "buy fewer colors / a smaller pack" doesn't map onto "you may not use this category at all" as precisely as it does for the other two. This is a minor residual gap in the instruction's fit, not a structural problem, since the underlying directive ("the category earns its slot, this specific form factor doesn't have to be bought in full") still gives the writer a coherent way to handle it (e.g., "pick one or two rolls you'll actually reach for, not the whole set"). Logged below for Stage 3b to verify the draft actually executes this for the sticker item specifically, rather than reusing the pens/tabs phrasing verbatim.
