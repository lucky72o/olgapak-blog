## Meta description (draft)

`Cornell, Outline, Mapping, or Charting? Compare the four main note-taking methods side by side and find the one that fits how you actually study or work.` (150 chars)

## Angle

Most guides dump a sequential list of five-to-seven methods and tell you to "pick your learning style." This one does the two things they don't: a genuine side-by-side comparison table, and a situation-first decision guide that starts from what you're doing (fast lecture, structured reading, a meeting, comparing facts) and points you to the method that fits. The throughline — straight from what students and note-takers actually say — is that no single method wins; the real skill is matching the method to the task and then refining your notes afterward, not transcribing everything live. Warm, first-person, honest about each method's trade-offs (no "Cornell has no downsides"), and it retires the debunked "learning styles" framing.
