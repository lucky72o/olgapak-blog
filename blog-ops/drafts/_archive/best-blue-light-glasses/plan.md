## Meta description (draft)

`Do blue light glasses actually work? Here's the honest evidence, plus 9 pairs worth buying for comfort, screen glare and late-night study.`

## Angle

Every roundup on page one hedges the "do they work" question in a paragraph and then sells you seven pairs anyway. This post does the opposite: it states plainly what the strongest evidence says (a 2023 Cochrane review of 17 trials found blue-light-filtering lenses may not reduce eye strain, and the American Academy of Ophthalmology does not recommend special eyewear for computer use), separates the daytime eye-strain claim from the evening-sleep claim because they have different evidence, and then helps the reader buy well for the reasons that survive that scrutiny: fit, comfort, a good anti-reflective coating, low colour distortion, prescription options, and a deliberately separate amber pair for night study. It also tells them the thing an optician will not: the blue-light coating is a high-margin upsell, and multiple people who sell lenses for a living say so themselves.

## Tone hooks

- Warm and level, never smug. The reader may already own a pair; the post must not make them feel stupid for buying one.
- The honest gate is the *service*, not a lecture. Say it plainly in two or three paragraphs, then get on with helping them choose.
- Name the discomfort out loud: yes, this post carries affiliate links, and it still tells you that habits beat lenses.
- Self-deprecating beat somewhere in the eye-strain section (Olga forgetting her own 20-20-20 alarm) per `blog-ops/profile/voice.md`.
- No scare-mongering about screens damaging eyes, in either direction.
