## Angle

Everyone writing about this topic states the same thing as settled fact — handwriting is better for memory — and almost nobody says where that comes from. Four of the seven competitors on this SERP assert it with no citation whatsoever, and the most academic-looking of them, a University of Wisconsin page, quietly disclaims its own post as opinion. Readers have already noticed the gap: one Reddit commenter called the article everyone cites "a blog post with a lot of suppositions stated as fact." So this post does the thing none of the competitors do — it names the actual study, says plainly what it measured and what it did not, and separates the two ideas that everyone collapses: **the advantage belongs to handwriting the motion, not to paper the material**, which is why a tablet and stylus sit on the handwriting side of the line. Then, instead of ending on "it depends on you" like the rest of the SERP, it hands the reader a decision rule drawn from what real note-takers actually do: choose by the stakes and the task, not by personality.

## Key sections (preview, becomes the outline in Phase 2)

1. **Digital vs paper notes at a glance** — the four-option comparison table (paper, laptop typing, tablet + stylus, hybrid) up front, so the reader gets the whole landscape on one screen before any argument. Mirrors the on-screen table that helps `research.com` rank, and mirrors this blog's own `note-taking-methods` opener.
2. **What the research actually says (and what it doesn't)** — names Mueller & Oppenheimer, states precisely what was measured (university students, lecture notes, conceptual questions, tested shortly after) and the three things it does not show. The honest core of the post.
3. **It's the handwriting, not the paper** — the distinction the whole topic collapses; puts tablet + stylus on the handwriting side and explains why that matters for the buying/choosing decision.
4. **Where digital genuinely wins** — search, backup, legibility, accessibility (the accessibility point comes from `utsc.utoronto.ca` and appears on no other competitor page), and matching the format of a digital exam.
5. **Where paper genuinely wins** — the friction that forces summarising, no notifications, easier review, diagram freedom.
6. **The hybrid workflow most people actually land on** — handwrite-then-digitize, the single most repeated real-world pattern in the Reddit threads; includes the "prettier notes are not better notes" trap.
7. **So which should you pick? A decision rule by stakes and task** — the concrete if-then framework, the section that beats the SERP's universal "it depends on you" ending.
8. **Conclusion / CTA** — durable framing, links the free AI tools with an action anchor.
9. FAQ

Not a listicle; no product/item list applies.

## Tone hooks

- **Confidence about uncertainty.** The post's authority comes from being the one page that says "here is exactly what this study showed, and here is what it didn't." That is a strength, delivered warmly, never a hedge or an apology.
- **Do not perform even-handedness.** The brief bars declaring a universal winner, but that is not the same as refusing to have a view. The view is: the motion matters more than the material, and the stakes decide the rest. Say it plainly.
- **Beginner-first translation.** "Encoding," "verbatim transcription," "conceptual recall" all get a plain-language gloss on first use, per `audience.md`.
- **Lived experience over survey.** The Master's-relearning anecdote should appear early and specifically (what actually happened in lectures), not as a generic credential line. Vary the wording from `cornell-note-taking-method` and `note-taking-methods`, which draw on the same anecdote bank.
- **Name the trap kindly.** The "prettier notes are not better notes" point should land as recognition, not scolding — this blog's reader may well be the person with the colour-coded iPad notes.
